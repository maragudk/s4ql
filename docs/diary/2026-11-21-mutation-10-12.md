# Diary: Mutation testing of slices 10, 11, and 12

Slices 10 (`if not exists` and `if exists`, #107 and #110), 11 (`default <literal>`, #109, #111, and #114), and 12 (a value on the left of a comparison, #116 and #118) are built and merged, and no round of the mutation tester `internal/sql/mutation` has run over their code. This task runs it over the code those pull requests wrote, kills every survivor with a test of what the code promises or lists it in `equivalent.txt` with a reason, and fixes any fault it shows. The branch is `mutation-10-12`, from `origin/main` at `25c39a7`.

## Step 1: The selection and the first runs

**Author:** mutation-10-12 builder

### Prompt Context

**Verbatim prompt:** (excerpt from the lead's brief) "Mutation testing of three slices of `docs/roadmap.md` that are built and merged but have not had it: [...] Scope: the code those three slices added or changed, found from the diffs of the pull requests named above (`gh pr diff <n> --name-only`, `git log`), in `internal/sql/stmt`, `internal/core`, `internal/engine`, and the driver in the root package. Where a file was touched only partly, mutate the functions the slices added or changed, and say in the report how you drew the line. Result wanted: every mutant in scope is killed by a test, or listed in `equivalent.txt` with a reason that a reader can check [...] do not change the generator, the profiles, the streams, `testdata/reached/` (`.kinds`, `.tsv`), or the digests. [...] The working copies of mutation runs go in `/Volumes/990/s4ql/mutation-10-12/` and nowhere else [...]"
**Interpretation:** Select the mutants on the lines that the seven pull requests wrote, by `git blame` as the round of slice 9 did, run each package on a copy of the tree under `/Volumes/990/s4ql/mutation-10-12/`, and classify every survivor.
**Inferred intent:** The same trust in slices 10 to 12 as in slices 1 to 9, without touching the generated checks that another builder is changing.

### What I did

- The scripts are in `/Volumes/990/s4ql/mutation-10-12/scripts/`: `pick.py` maps every commit of each pull request (the range `M^1..M^2` of its merge `M`, and `M` itself) to its number, blames every file of the listing at `HEAD`, and keeps the mutants whose line one of those commits wrote; `regex.py` turns the kept lines into a `-run` expression of `file:line` per package; `copy.sh` copies the worktree without its repository to a tree there; `run.sh` runs the tool on such a tree with `GOCACHE`, `TMPDIR`, and `-gocache` there too.
- `-list` over `stmt`, the core, the engine, the format, and the driver gave 17,224 mutants; the selection kept 1,069: `stmt` 264 (`recognizer.createTable`, `createIndex`, `drop`, `ifExists`, `notIf`, `column`, `defaultValue`, `literal`, `sign`, `takes`, `condition`, `conditionColumn`, `isCondition`, `depth`, `fieldNames`, and `Operator.Swapped`), the engine 413 (`Conn.nothingToWrite`, `Conn.write`, `Conn.writeIn`, `buildSink` with its defaults, `buildCreateIndex`, `buildDropIndex`, `nothing`, `replaceTable`, `Format`, `ifClause`, `NewPlan`, `report.doesNothing`, `report.end`, `report.of`, `tooLongForCache`, `coreTx.createTable`, `coreTx.createIndex`, `fit`, `build.conditions`, and `rowsOf`), the core 315 (all of `default.go`, and lines of `conn.load`, `newTable`, `table.createSQL`, `table.createTable`, `cacheVersion`, `conn.create`, `conn.createIndex`, and `Tx.CreateTable`), the format 67 (`checkDefault`, and lines of `checkCreateTable`, `checkStored`, and `checkStoredValue`), and the driver 10 (`check`).
- Started `stmt` with 2 workers, the engine with 3 and `-factor 5`, and the core with 2 and `-factor 3`, each with `-first` of the tests of its slices, on the copy `tree1`.

### Why

The format is outside the brief's list of packages, but #111 is "the commit format and the storage core" and `docs/todo/slice-11.md` names `checkDefault` among what is left to mutate; its tests take seconds.

### What worked

- The blame selection matched the counts that the tool then reported for each package.

### What didn't work

- The first name of the selection script, `select.py`, shadowed Python's module `select`, which `subprocess` imports: `AttributeError: partially initialized module 'subprocess' has no attribute 'run' (most likely due to a circular import)`. It is `pick.py` now.
- A shell command that chained `rsync --exclude .git` with other commands was refused as one that names git in a form too complex to verify; the copy is a script of its own.

### What I learned

- The tool plans every stage again in each invocation, so a run of the core cannot be cut into foreground shards of ten minutes; the runs go in the background and are watched.

### What was tricky

- Lines that a later pull request rewrote (#112's strict load in `conn.load`, slice 13 in `buildSink`) are not in the selection, which follows `git blame`.

### What warrants review

- The selection by line: a function that the slices changed has only its changed lines mutated.

### Future work

- The survivors of each run.

## Step 2: The survivors of the first runs, and what closes them

**Author:** mutation-10-12 builder

### Prompt Context

**Verbatim prompt:** (from the brief) "A survivor is killed by a test of the behaviour it breaks, written as a test of what the code promises, not as a test of the mutant. No mutant is left \"survived\" without a decision. If a survivor shows a real fault in s4ql, fix it with a test that pins it and say so in the report [...]"
**Interpretation:** Take each survivor as it comes, check a test against its mutant at once, and keep the decisions for the rerun on the final code.
**Inferred intent:** Every mutant of the three slices accounted for, with tests that read as promises.

### What I did

- `/Volumes/990/s4ql/mutation-10-12/scripts/mut.py` applies one mutant to a copy of a file and runs a test through `go test -overlay`, so each new test was checked against its mutant in seconds, without touching the worktree or the running copies.
- The first runs ended: `stmt` 264 mutants in 49 min, the engine 413 in 1 h 29 min, the core 315 in 1 h 40 min, the format 67 in 22 min, and the driver 10 in 3 min, at a load of 11 to 16 on 10 cores. `stmt` left 11 survivors, the engine 34 (5 listed already), the core 21, the format 1 (listed), the driver none.
- New tests, each of a promise: `TestRecognize` takes a create table named by the bare word IF without IF NOT EXISTS, from a tree that the parser never gives, as it did for a create index; `TestCheck` refuses ON CONFLICT before a second DEFAULT at the ON; `TestEngine_write` gives the event of a statement that does nothing, through the reader of its own and through the write transaction, with its position and no parent, and takes the write path where the reader of its own cannot be opened; `TestConn_writeIn` releases a statement that does nothing in a transaction without an annotation and without a rollback to its savepoint; `TestBuild` refuses a plan whose default is a nil blob literal with the internal error; `TestFromCore` maps the core's `ErrTooLongForCache` of a table and of an index to "not supported" by its name, on a real core, and any other refusal to an internal error; `TestEngine_reading` draws no direction for a statement of the clauses; `TestOpen_cache_defaults` pins the cache's statement of a table whose key is two columns, which declares neither key column not null; `TestTx_tooBig` pins the whole message of a table and of an index too long for the cache; `TestDefaultSQL` refuses every literal for a column of no known type.
- Code that nothing needs, removed: in `stmt`, the guards `d.Name.Present() && r.isNext(d.Name)` and the `unexpected` for a name or parentheses in `recognizer.defaultValue`, since a name is the token after the DEFAULT that the caller took and `recognizer.value` names a token that is no literal; and `value != ""` in `recognizer.conditionColumn`, whose one caller with "" has checked that the side is no value. In the core, the two `if`s around `proto.CloneOf` of a default in `newTable` and `table.createTable`, since the clone of none is none; `pk == 0` in the nullability of `conn.load`, since a key column that takes NULL is refused above it; and the quote and `x'` checks of a text and a blob in `parseDefault`, which the round trip at its end makes.
- The equivalent ones are in a section of `/internal/sql/mutation/equivalent.txt` of their own: the variants of the planted bugs `replaceExisting` and `nullDefault`, `nothing.run`, which no caller reaches, the error beside a sink of `buildSink`, the length check of a default, which is never over the limit, the version of the cache, the precision of `strconv.FormatFloat` and the bit size of `strconv.ParseFloat`, the errors of `strconv.ParseInt` and `hex.DecodeString` that the round trip of `parseDefault` refuses anyway, and the kind test of `withoutNegativeZero`.
- Slice 12 left no survivor but one in `conditionColumn`, removed above.

### Why

Removing code that nothing needs, where the README of the runner asks for it, leaves fewer reasons for a reader to check than a list of equivalent mutants would.

### What worked

- The overlay checker: 20 mutants checked against their new tests, all killed, before any rerun.

### What didn't work

- The first version of the test of directions used `&drawHost{draws: ...}` without a host, and the engine's report panicked at `Monotonic`: `panic: runtime error: invalid memory address or nil pointer dereference` in `(*drawHost).Monotonic`. It embeds `host.New()` now.
- `simstore.New(host.New(), ...)` does not compile, `host.Host does not implement simstore.Host (missing method Trace)`; the test of the storage uses `sim.NewFree(sim.Buggify{})`, as `tx_test.go` does.
- A foreground `wait` of 540 s was moved to the background after 180 s by the harness; the waits are 170 s since.

### What I learned

- `Conn.nothingToWrite` passing `reading(true)` would take a draw of the seed of directions from every later query: the test of no draw is the one way to see it.

### What was tricky

- Telling the planted bugs' variants from real gaps: the variants still plant a wrong statement that the simulation finds, so they follow the earlier entries of planted bugs.

### What warrants review

- The reason of `buildSink remove statement: continue =>`, which rests on the core holding no table whose statement in the cache is longer than the limit.
- The simplified `parseDefault`, which now leaves the spelling of the quotes to its round trip.

### Future work

- The rerun of the survivors and of this round's lines on the final code.

## Step 3: The reruns, the review, and the checks

**Author:** mutation-10-12 builder

### Prompt Context

**Verbatim prompt:** (from the brief) "Run `make test` and `make lint` before handing in. [...] When done: self-review, push, open a pull request with `gh pr create --reviewer markuswustenberg` [...] Do not merge it and do not wait for CI."
**Interpretation:** Rerun every survivor and every mutant on a line that the round changed on the final code, review the branch with the code-review skill, and run the checks.
**Inferred intent:** Numbers of the code as it is merged, and a branch that a reviewer can trust.

### What I did

- `/Volumes/990/s4ql/mutation-10-12/scripts/rerun.py` lists, on the new code, the mutants on lines of this round's commits (`25c39a7..HEAD` given to `pick.py` as round 0) and those whose key survived the first runs, and names the survivor keys that the new code no longer has. The reruns on the copy `tree2`: `stmt` 18 mutants, 2 not compiled, 16 killed; the engine 34, 14 killed, 20 survived and all listed; the core 32, 3 not compiled, 21 killed, 8 survived and all listed. `scripts/tally.py` joins the first runs with the reruns by slice and package; the tables are in `/docs/todo/slice-10.md`, `/docs/todo/slice-11.md`, `/docs/todo/slice-12.md`, and `/internal/sql/mutation/README.md`.
- The code-review skill (medium) found one real regression: with the check of the parenthesis gone from `recognizer.defaultValue`, `default (0x10)` gave `not supported: hex literal` where `main` gives `DEFAULT of an expression`, and so did `(current_time)`, `(1e999)`, `(1_000)`, and an integer outside 64 bits, since `recognizer.value` refuses the literal inside before the take of it meets the parenthesis. The check of the parenthesis is back, before the value is read, and `TestCheck` has `default (0x10)` and `default (current_time)`; only the guards of the name stay removed. A rerun of the four lines of `defaultValue` and `conditionColumn` that the round changed (`tree3`) made 20 mutants: 2 not compiled, 18 killed. Its two nits were left: a future caller of `conditionColumn` with "" and a value would give an empty name, which the doc comment rules out, and the refusal of other spellings by the round trip of `parseDefault` is pinned already by the refused texts of `TestDefaultSQL`.
- `make lint`: 0 issues. `make test`: passes. The new tests pass 20 times under `-race`, the engine's and the core's, and 20 times without for `TestRecognize`.

### Why

The first runs counted the survivors on the code as it was; only a run on the final code shows that the new tests kill them, and that the lines the round wrote have no survivor of their own.

### What worked

- The review read the removal of a guard against inputs that no test had, which the mutation runs could not: the survivor was equivalent only for the literals that the tests use.

### What didn't work

- My first reading of the survivors of `defaultValue` took the check of the parenthesis for code that nothing needs, from the four survivors alone; it was not, for literals that the value refuses for itself.

### What I learned

- A survivor of a guard before a call that refuses things of its own is equivalent only if the call refuses nothing that the guard would have named differently; the cases of the test have to cover what the call refuses.

### What was tricky

- Keeping the counts apart: survivors killed by new tests, survivors in code that went, and those listed, across five first runs and three reruns.

### What warrants review

- The new sections of `/internal/sql/mutation/equivalent.txt`.
- The restored check of the parenthesis in `recognizer.defaultValue`.

### Future work

- Long fuzz runs of the three slices, in the todo of each.
