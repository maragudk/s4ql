# Slice 15: a scalar subquery in a WHERE

The decisions and notes of the builders of [slice 15 of the roadmap](../roadmap.md).

## Agreed with Markus on 2026-11-24

- **The slice.** The scalar subquery of goqite's receive, `update goqite set timeout = ?, received = received + 1 where queue = ? and id = (select id from goqite where queue = ? and ? >= timeout and received < ? order by priority desc, created limit 1) returning id, body, received`, without the arithmetic in SET (`received + 1`), which is not in this slice.

## The statement pass

- **Decisions of the statement pass of slice 15, by its builder on 2026-11-24, built, for the lead to confirm.** Each fact of SQLite here was measured with the pinned shell (3.54.1), and the tests of the pass check it against SQLite: `checkSubqueries` in `stmt_test.go` on every supported text with a subquery, and the subtests of `TestCheck` named below. [The README of the pass](../../internal/sql/stmt/README.md) has the forms token for token.
  - **Where a subquery is supported.** As the value of a comparison of the WHERE of a select, an update, or a delete, on either side of its column, with each of the six operators and their eight spellings, in every place where a `?` is supported there today. The recognizer reads the value of every comparison with one function, `recognizer.conditionValue`, so the operators and the two sides are uniform, as the brief asked. The agreed case is `=` on the right, and goqite's receive is it.
  - **The typed form.** `stmt.Select`, `stmt.Update`, and `stmt.Delete` have a field `Subqueries []stmt.Select`: the subqueries of the WHERE in the order of the text, or nil. The value of a condition names one by `stmt.Term.Subquery`, its number from 1 in that list, as `Term.Parameter` names an argument; its `Value` is nil and its `Parameter` 0, and its `Span` is from the opening parenthesis to the end of the closing one. A pointer in `Term` was not taken: no statement holds a pointer, the statements are compared and printed by value (`%#v` in the tests and the generator), and a select inside a select needs a pointer or a list. The inner select is a `stmt.Select` of the same kind as a statement, with its `Span` from its SELECT to its last token, without the parentheses, `Columns` of one name or nil for `*`, its `Where`, `OrderBy`, and `Limit`, and `Subqueries` nil. Its names have their offsets, as every name has.
  - **The parameters** of a subquery are numbered with those of the statement in the order of the text, as SQLite numbers them: in goqite's receive, the `?` of SET is 1, that of `queue = ?` 2, the three of the subquery 3, 4, and 5. The recognizer now counts the parameters it has taken in one counter, `recognizer.parameters`, in place of the count that each function was given, so there is one rule for the whole statement. `Parameters()` of a statement counts those of its subqueries too. A `?` of the LIMIT of a select comes after those of its WHERE and of the subqueries in it.
  - **One result column.** A subquery with a second result column is not supported, as `subquery of more than one column`, at the second column. SQLite refuses such a statement in every database: with `row value misused` (in a select, an update, and a delete alike), also when the list names a column that the table lacks, and with `no such table` first in a select whose subquery's table is missing. So s4ql is stricter than SQLite only in the error it gives, not in what runs. It follows `index beyond SQLite's limit`, an error that SQLite gives once it has found the table and that the pass gives as not supported. `*` is supported, since it is one column on a table of one column, which the pass cannot know; `*` with another column is `* and another result column`, the rule of the select list.
  - **Refused, each by name, at its first token unless said otherwise:** a subquery in the WHERE of a subquery, on either side, as `subquery in a subquery`, at its parenthesis; a subquery after a `-`, as `subquery after a sign`, at the parenthesis, and after a `+` as `+ sign`, at the `+`, as for a parameter; `in (select ...)` and `not in (select ...)` as `IN`; `exists (select ...)` as `EXISTS` and `not exists` as `NOT operator`; a subquery in FROM, also in the FROM of a subquery, as `subquery in FROM`; a compound select in the parentheses (UNION, UNION ALL, EXCEPT, INTERSECT) as `compound SELECT`, at its operator; a subquery in the select list and in RETURNING as `result column other than a column or *`; in SET and in VALUES as `subquery`; in ORDER BY as `ordering term other than a column`; in LIMIT as `LIMIT of an expression`; in a DEFAULT, which only `default ((select 1))` can write, as `DEFAULT of an expression` (`default (select 1)` is SQLite's syntax error); a subquery alone as a condition as `subquery`; a subquery before IS as `value before IS`, and after IS as `IS with a value other than NULL`, which `v is (select ...)` was named `subquery` before; a subquery compared with a literal, a parameter, or a subquery as `value compared with a value`; a subquery in parentheses as `parentheses`; and with COLLATE as `COLLATE`. Inside the parentheses everything that a select refuses keeps its name: WITH, DISTINCT, GROUP BY, an alias, a schema, a join, OFFSET, OR, a function, a column with the name of its table, a numbered parameter, a select without FROM (`SELECT without FROM`, at the closing parenthesis), and VALUES.
  - **The depth.** SQLite counts the height of a subquery twice. While it parses, a subquery is one level more than its select, and the comparison one more than the subquery: `k = (select k from u where <n conditions>)` is too deep from n = 998, at the token after the comparison, from 999 at the closing parenthesis, and from 1,000 at the AND of the inner WHERE, all as `WHERE deeper than SQLite's limit`. When SQLite resolves the names, once it has found the tables, it adds the height of the WHERE of the statement and that of the WHERE of the subquery: so n is at most 498, and 497 when the comparison is in an AND. SQLite gives `Expression tree is too large (maximum depth 1000)` then on a database with the tables, and `no such table` on an empty one. The pass refuses it as `WHERE deeper than SQLite's limit` at the subquery, by the rule of `index beyond SQLite's limit`. A subtest of `TestCheck`, "should take a subquery as deep as SQLite allows, and not one deeper", checks both edges against SQLite, with the tables. So `parameter beyond SQLite's limit` can now occur in a WHERE: many subqueries of few conditions each hold more than 32,766 `?`.
  - **A correlated subquery is not detected here.** SQLite looks a name of a subquery up in its own table first, and then in the table of the statement, without an error: `select * from t where v = (select v from u)` gives the rows of `t` where `u` has a row, when `u` has no column `v`, and `order by v` in the subquery orders by the `v` of the statement. Whether a name is the subquery's depends on the tables; see the open item in [the SQL front end](sql-front-end.md). A column with the name of its table, `t.v`, and a column compared with a column, `x = v`, are refused already, so the only names that can be correlated are the bare result column, the column of a condition, and a column of ORDER BY of the subquery.
  - **Until the engine runs slice 15,** `engine.subqueryRefusal`, called in `engine.check`, gives a select, an update, or a delete with a subquery the error that the pass gave it before: `s4ql: not supported: subquery`, at the opening parenthesis of its first subquery. A text with a token in a subquery that the pass refuses gets the pass's error for that token: `(select k + 1 from u)` gives `result column other than a column or *`. `engine.NewPlan` panics for such a statement. The driver's `check` calls `engine.Check` after `stmt.Check` again, as it did for slice 14 until its engine step, so `Prepare` refuses the text. `TestCheck_subquery`, `TestConn_subquery`, and `TestPair_subquery` in `internal/engine/subquery_test.go` and `TestPrepare` in `conn_test.go` pin it; `subqueried` there lets the differential tests take the refusal for a text that the pass supports, and skips the rules of values and `pair.everyPath` for it (`TestPair_subquery` found that `everyPath` panicked on such a text, in `stmttest.SelectText`).
  - **The generator.** `stmttest.Generator.Subquery`, 0 by default: with it, the value of one comparison in so many of a WHERE at random is a subquery, on either side, of the table of the statement one time in two (where the pool of `SelectTable` has its name), of one column or, one time in eight, `*`, with a WHERE, ORDER BY, and LIMIT as a select at random has them. With 0 it draws as before: a dump of 10,320 lines, `%#v` of `Generator.Statement` and `Generator.CallOf` of every kind in three settings, one with hooks, and of `Sequence.Next`, over 30 seeds, was the same byte for byte before the change and after it. The pass's own tests set it to 4. There is no hook and no area: `Sequence` writes no subquery, and the engine's and the driver's tests set no `Subquery`.
  - **The numbers that the pass's tests pin** moved only by the generator's draws: `TestGenerate_mutants` from 45 to 35 syntax errors and from 167 to 139 not supported where SQLite does not say, and `TestGenerate_nearMisses` from 1,983 to 2,275. A tally by SQLite's message found create tables only (`duplicate column name`, `unknown table option`, `AUTOINCREMENT is only allowed on an INTEGER PRIMARY KEY`, `no such collation sequence`, `has more than one primary key`, `default value of column [x] is not constant`); with `Subquery` 0 the old near misses are 1,983 again, and the new form `default ((select 1))` adds the 253 of `default value of column [x] is not constant`. No excused text has a subquery. The corpus, its mutants, and the grammar keep their numbers.
- **Facts of SQLite for the builder of the engine of slice 15, measured by the builder of its statement pass on 2026-11-24.** The subtest "should answer for a subquery what SQLite does with its errors, its value, and its LIMIT" of `TestCheck` pins each, and `checkSubqueries` checks the value and the state before on every supported text.
  - **The value** is that of the one column of the first row that the subquery gives, or NULL without a row, so the comparison then chooses nothing. With several rows and no ORDER BY, SQLite gives the first in its order of reading, which is key order on a plain scan. `limit 0` gives NULL.
  - **Once, from the state before.** SQLite computes the value once, before the statement changes any row, also when the subquery reads the table that an update or a delete changes: on `t` of three rows with `v = 0`, `update t set v = 9 where v = (select v from t order by k limit 1)` changes all three.
  - **Lazily.** SQLite computes it only when it needs it: with `limit ?` bound to NULL, which is `datatype mismatch`, `select * from t where v = (select k from u limit ?)` on an empty `t` gives no error, and, measured with the shell only, `where k = 5 and v = (...)` none when no row has the key 5, while `where k = (...)` gives the error also on an empty `t`, since SQLite needs the value to search the key. So whether an error of the subquery shows depends on SQLite's plan.
  - **LIMIT.** SQLite reads the LIMIT X of a scalar subquery as LIMIT (X <> 0): `limit ?` with `'x'`, `-1`, `1.5`, or `x'00'` gives the first row, with 0 or 0.0 none, and with NULL `datatype mismatch`. A literal of the pass is an integer from 0, for which the two readings agree. The rule for an argument of `limit ?` of slice 13, an integer from 0 and nothing else, would be stricter than SQLite here; which the engine gives is for its builder.
  - **The order of the errors.** No such table first: in a select SQLite finds every table of the statement before any column, that of the statement and then those of the subqueries in the order of the text, so `select * from t where nope = (select k from nope2)` and `select nope from t where k = (select k from nope2)` give `no such table: nope2`. In an update and a delete it finds the table of a subquery only when its walk over WHERE reaches it: `update t set v = 1 where nope = 1 and k = (select k from nope2)` gives `no such column: nope`, and `... where k = (select k from nope2) returning nope` gives `no such table: nope2`. The columns come in the order of the text, those of a subquery where the subquery is: `k = (select nope from u) and nope2 = 1` gives `no such column: nope`, and `nope2 = 1 and k = (select nope from u)` gives `no such column: nope2`. A missing column of the subquery's ORDER BY is `no such column` too.
  - **`*` of more than one column** is `row value misused` in a select, on either side, and `sub-select returns 2 columns - expected 1`, with the number of the columns, in an update and a delete, but for `!=` and `<>` with the subquery on the left, where it is `row value misused` too. It comes after the names of WHERE (`delete from t where k = (select * from u) and nope = 1` gives `no such column: nope`) and before those of RETURNING.
  - **Read-only,** measured with the shell only. Under `pragma query_only` a select with a subquery runs, and an update with one gives `attempt to write a readonly database`.

## Stricter than SQLite or different from it, for the lead to tell Markus

- A subquery of two or more named columns is not supported, where SQLite gives `row value misused`, or `no such table` first: an error either way.
- A subquery whose WHERE is deeper than SQLite resolves is not supported, where SQLite gives `Expression tree is too large` once it has found the tables, and `no such table` on an empty database.
- `v is (select ...)` is now `IS with a value other than NULL` where it was `subquery`, and a text whose first refused token is inside a subquery gets the error for that token where it got `subquery`; both are refused, as before.
- Every form that SQLite takes and the slice does not name stays refused, as the brief listed: IN, EXISTS, a subquery outside a comparison of a WHERE, a nested subquery, a compound, and a subquery after a sign.
