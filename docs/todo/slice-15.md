# Slice 15: a scalar subquery in a WHERE

The decisions and notes of the builders of [slice 15 of the roadmap](../roadmap.md).

## Agreed with Markus on 2026-11-24

- **The slice.** The scalar subquery of goqite's receive, `update goqite set timeout = ?, received = received + 1 where queue = ? and id = (select id from goqite where queue = ? and ? >= timeout and received < ? order by priority desc, created limit 1) returning id, body, received`, without the arithmetic in SET (`received + 1`), which is not in this slice.

## The statement pass

- **Decisions of the statement pass of slice 15, by its builder on 2026-11-24, built, for the lead to confirm.** Each fact of SQLite here was measured with the pinned shell (3.54.1), and the tests of the pass check it against SQLite: `checkSubqueries` in `stmt_test.go` on every supported text with a subquery, and the subtests of `TestCheck` named below. [The README of the pass](../../internal/sql/stmt/README.md) has the forms token for token.
  - **Where a subquery is supported.** As the value of a comparison of the WHERE of a select, an update, or a delete, on either side of its column, with each of the six operators and their eight spellings, in every place where a `?` is supported there today. The recognizer reads the value of every comparison with one function, `recognizer.conditionValue`, so the operators and the two sides are uniform, as the brief asked. The agreed case is `=` on the right, and goqite's receive is it.
  - **The typed form.** `stmt.Select`, `stmt.Update`, and `stmt.Delete` have a field `Subqueries []stmt.Select`: the subqueries of the WHERE in the order of the text, or nil. The value of a condition names one by `stmt.Term.Subquery`, its number from 1 in that list, as `Term.Parameter` names an argument; its `Value` is nil and its `Parameter` 0, and its `Span` is from the opening parenthesis to the end of the closing one. A pointer in `Term` was not taken: no statement holds a pointer, the statements are compared and printed by value (`%#v` in the tests and the generator), and a select inside a select needs a pointer or a list. The inner select is a `stmt.Select` of the same kind as a statement, with its `Span` from its SELECT to its last token, without the parentheses, `Columns` of one name or nil for `*`, its `Where`, `OrderBy`, and `Limit`, and `Subqueries` nil. Its names have their offsets, as every name has.
  - **The parameters** of a subquery are numbered with those of the statement in the order of the text, as SQLite numbers them: in goqite's receive, the `?` of SET is 1, that of `queue = ?` 2, the three of the subquery 3, 4, and 5. The recognizer now counts the parameters it has taken in one counter, `recognizer.parameters`, in place of the count that each function was given, so there is one rule for the whole statement. `Parameters()` of a statement counts those of its subqueries too. A `?` of the LIMIT of a select comes after those of its WHERE and of the subqueries in it.
  - **One result column.** A subquery with a second result column is not supported, as `subquery of more than one column`, at the second column. SQLite refuses such a statement in every database: with `row value misused` (in a select, an update, and a delete alike), also when the list names a column that the table lacks, and with `no such table` first in a select whose subquery's table is missing. So s4ql is stricter than SQLite only in the error it gives, not in what runs. It follows `index beyond SQLite's limit`, an error that SQLite gives once it has found the table and that the pass gives as not supported. `*` is supported, since it is one column on a table of one column, which the pass cannot know; `*` with another column is `* and another result column`, the rule of the select list.
  - **Refused, each by name, at its first token unless said otherwise:** a subquery in the WHERE of a subquery, on either side, as `subquery in a subquery`, at its parenthesis; a subquery after a `-`, as `subquery after a sign`, at the parenthesis, and after a `+` as `+ sign`, at the `+`, as for a parameter; `in (select ...)` and `not in (select ...)` as `IN`; `exists (select ...)` as `EXISTS` and `not exists` as `NOT operator`; a subquery in FROM, also in the FROM of a subquery, as `subquery in FROM`; a compound select in the parentheses (UNION, UNION ALL, EXCEPT, INTERSECT) as `compound SELECT`, at its operator; a subquery in the select list and in RETURNING as `result column other than a column or *`; in SET and in VALUES as `subquery`; in ORDER BY as `ordering term other than a column`; in LIMIT as `LIMIT of an expression`; in a DEFAULT, which only `default ((select 1))` can write, as `DEFAULT of an expression` (`default (select 1)` is SQLite's syntax error); a subquery alone as a condition as `subquery`; a subquery before IS as `value before IS`, and after IS as `IS with a value other than NULL`, which `v is (select ...)` was named `subquery` before; a subquery compared with a literal, a parameter, or a subquery as `value compared with a value`; a subquery in parentheses as `parentheses`; and with COLLATE as `COLLATE`. Inside the parentheses everything that a select refuses keeps its name: WITH, DISTINCT, GROUP BY, an alias, a schema, a join, OFFSET, OR, a function, a column with the name of its table, a numbered parameter, a select without FROM (`SELECT without FROM`, at the closing parenthesis), and VALUES.
  - **The depth.** SQLite counts the height of a subquery twice. While it parses, a subquery is one level more than its select, and the comparison one more than the subquery: `k = (select k from u where <n conditions>)` is too deep from n = 998, at the token after the comparison, from 999 at the closing parenthesis, and from 1,000 at the AND of the inner WHERE, all as `WHERE deeper than SQLite's limit`. When SQLite resolves the names, once it has found the tables, it adds the height of the WHERE of the statement and that of the WHERE of the subquery: so n is at most 498, and 497 when the comparison is in an AND. SQLite gives `Expression tree is too large (maximum depth 1000)` then on a database with the tables, and `no such table` on an empty one. The pass refuses it as `WHERE deeper than SQLite's limit` at the subquery, by the rule of `index beyond SQLite's limit`. A subtest of `TestCheck`, "should take a subquery as deep as SQLite allows, and not one deeper", checks both edges against SQLite, with the tables. So `parameter beyond SQLite's limit` can now occur in a WHERE: many subqueries of few conditions each hold more than 32,766 `?`.
  - **A correlated subquery is not detected here.** SQLite looks a name of a subquery up in its own table first, and then in the table of the statement, without an error: `select * from t where v = (select v from u)` gives the rows of `t` where `u` has a row, when `u` has no column `v`, and `order by v` in the subquery orders by the `v` of the statement. Whether a name is the subquery's depends on the tables, so the engine refuses a correlated subquery; see [the engine and the driver](#the-engine-and-the-driver). A column with the name of its table, `t.v`, and a column compared with a column, `x = v`, are refused already, so the only names that can be correlated are the bare result column, the column of a condition, and a column of ORDER BY of the subquery.
  - **Until the engine runs slice 15,** `engine.subqueryRefusal`, called in `engine.check`, gives a select, an update, or a delete with a subquery the error that the pass gave it before: `s4ql: not supported: subquery`, at the opening parenthesis of its first subquery. A text with a token in a subquery that the pass refuses gets the pass's error for that token: `(select k + 1 from u)` gives `result column other than a column or *`. `engine.NewPlan` panics for such a statement. The driver's `check` calls `engine.Check` after `stmt.Check` again, as it did for slice 14 until its engine step, so `Prepare` refuses the text. `TestCheck_subquery`, `TestConn_subquery`, and `TestPair_subquery` in `internal/engine/subquery_test.go` and `TestPrepare` in `conn_test.go` pin it; `subqueried` there lets the differential tests take the refusal for a text that the pass supports, and skips the rules of values and `pair.everyPath` for it (`TestPair_subquery` found that `everyPath` panicked on such a text, in `stmttest.SelectText`).
  - **The generator.** `stmttest.Generator.Subquery`, 0 by default: with it, the value of one comparison in so many of a WHERE at random is a subquery, on either side, of the table of the statement one time in two (where the pool of `SelectTable` has its name), of one column or, one time in eight, `*`, with a WHERE, ORDER BY, and LIMIT as a select at random has them. With 0 it draws as before: a dump of 10,320 lines, `%#v` of `Generator.Statement` and `Generator.CallOf` of every kind in three settings, one with hooks, and of `Sequence.Next`, over 30 seeds, was the same byte for byte before the change and after it. The pass's own tests set it to 4. There is no hook and no area: `Sequence` writes no subquery, and the engine's and the driver's tests set no `Subquery`.
  - **The numbers that the pass's tests pin** moved only by the generator's draws: `TestGenerate_mutants` from 45 to 35 syntax errors and from 167 to 139 not supported where SQLite does not say, and `TestGenerate_nearMisses` from 1,983 to 2,275. A tally by SQLite's message found create tables only (`duplicate column name`, `unknown table option`, `AUTOINCREMENT is only allowed on an INTEGER PRIMARY KEY`, `no such collation sequence`, `has more than one primary key`, `default value of column [x] is not constant`); with `Subquery` 0 the old near misses are 1,983 again, and the new form `default ((select 1))` adds the 253 of `default value of column [x] is not constant`. No excused text has a subquery. The corpus, its mutants, and the grammar keep their numbers.
- **Facts of SQLite for the builder of the engine of slice 15, measured by the builder of its statement pass on 2026-11-24.** The subtest "should answer for a subquery what SQLite does with its errors, its value, and its LIMIT" of `TestCheck` pins each, and `checkSubqueries` checks the value and the state before on every supported text.
  - **The value** is that of the one column of the first row that the subquery gives, or NULL without a row, so the comparison then chooses nothing. With several rows and no ORDER BY, SQLite gives the first in its order of reading, which is key order on a plain scan. `limit 0` gives NULL.
  - **Once, from the state before.** SQLite computes the value once, before the statement changes any row, also when the subquery reads the table that an update or a delete changes: on `t` of three rows with `v = 0`, `update t set v = 9 where v = (select v from t order by k limit 1)` changes all three.
  - **Lazily.** SQLite computes it only when it needs it: with `limit ?` bound to NULL, which is `datatype mismatch`, `select * from t where v = (select k from u limit ?)` on an empty `t` gives no error, and, measured with the shell only, `where k = 5 and v = (...)` none when no row has the key 5, while `where k = (...)` gives the error also on an empty `t`, since SQLite needs the value to search the key. So whether an error of the subquery shows depends on SQLite's plan.
  - **LIMIT.** SQLite reads the LIMIT X of a scalar subquery as LIMIT (X <> 0): `limit ?` with `'x'`, `-1`, `1.5`, or `x'00'` gives the first row, with 0 or 0.0 none, and with NULL `datatype mismatch`. A literal of the pass is an integer from 0, for which the two readings agree. The rule for an argument of `limit ?` of slice 13, an integer from 0 and nothing else, would be stricter than SQLite here; which the engine gives is for its builder.
  - **The order of the errors.** No such table first: in a select SQLite finds every table of the statement before any column, that of the statement and then those of the subqueries in the order of the text, so `select * from t where nope = (select k from nope2)` and `select nope from t where k = (select k from nope2)` give `no such table: nope2`. In an update and a delete it finds the table of a subquery only when its walk over WHERE reaches it: `update t set v = 1 where nope = 1 and k = (select k from nope2)` gives `no such column: nope`, and `... where k = (select k from nope2) returning nope` gives `no such table: nope2`. The columns come in the order of the text, those of a subquery where the subquery is: `k = (select nope from u) and nope2 = 1` gives `no such column: nope`, and `nope2 = 1 and k = (select nope from u)` gives `no such column: nope2`. A missing column of the subquery's ORDER BY is `no such column` too.
  - **`*` of more than one column** is `row value misused` in a select, on either side, and `sub-select returns 2 columns - expected 1`, with the number of the columns, in an update and a delete, but for `!=` and `<>` with the subquery on the left, where it is `row value misused` too. It comes after the names of WHERE (`delete from t where k = (select * from u) and nope = 1` gives `no such column: nope`) and before those of RETURNING.
  - **Read-only,** measured with the shell only. Under `pragma query_only` a select with a subquery runs, and an update with one gives `attempt to write a readonly database`.

## The engine and the driver

- **Decisions of the engine step of slice 15, by its builder on 2026-11-24, built, for the lead to confirm.** Each fact of SQLite here was measured with the pinned shell, and `TestEngine_subquery` in `internal/engine/subquery_test.go`, `TestNewPlan` in `plan_test.go`, the subtest of the subquery's event of `TestEngine_report` in `engine_internal_test.go`, and `TestSubquery` in `conn_test.go` of the root package check them against SQLite. The code is in `internal/engine/subquery.go`.
  - **The refusals of the statement pass are gone.** `engine.subqueryRefusal`, the panic in `NewPlan`, `TestCheck_subquery`, `TestConn_subquery`, `TestPair_subquery`, and the exceptions in `pair.step` and `pair.everyPath` are removed. The driver's `check` calls `stmt.Check` alone again, as after the engine step of slice 14. The simulator's SQL box still calls `engine.Check` after `stmt.Check`; another session owns the simulator, and the call now refuses nothing that the pass does not. `TestEngine_box` in `internal/simulator/box_test.go` expected an update with a subquery to be refused, and now expects it `committed`.
  - **The plan.** `Query`, `Update`, and `Delete` have a field `Subqueries []*Query`: one `*Query` for each subquery, in the order of the text, of the shape of a select (a `Project` over a `Limit` over an `Order` over a `Filter` over a `Scan`), whose own `Subqueries` is nil. The value of the condition is a new expression, `*Subquery`, with `Number`, from 1, and `Offset`, the offset of the opening parenthesis, for messages. `Format` prints the value as `(subquery 1)`, and after the nodes of the statement a line `subquery 1` with the nodes of its query indented under it. Goqite's receive prints as:
    ```
    update goqite set timeout = ?1 returning id, body, received
      filter queue = ?2 and id = (subquery 1)
        scan goqite
      subquery 1
        project id
          limit 1
            order by priority desc, created
              filter queue = ?3 and timeout <= ?4 and received < ?5
                scan goqite
    ```
  - **Once, before the statement, from the same state.** The engine computes the value of each subquery once for each run of the statement, in the build of its operators (`build.compute`). It does so after every check of the statement (names, lengths, and types), and before the statement chooses its own access and reads or changes a row: in the read transaction of a select, and in the write transaction, or the savepoint in a transaction, of an update or a delete. So an update or a delete of the table that its subquery reads sees the rows as they were before the statement: `update t set w = 5 where w = (select w from t order by k limit 1)` changes every row with that `w`. A statement that can give or change no row whatever its subqueries give computes none, as SQLite runs none for it, and each value is NULL: a select with LIMIT 0, one with another condition that is never true (`never`), and an update or a delete in a read-only transaction, which gives `attempt to write a readonly database` after the errors of its names and reads nothing. Its event then has `statement.subqueries` and none of the attributes of their reading. (The review of this step found the reads of a refused write and of LIMIT 0.)
  - **The access of a subquery.** `chooseAccess` gives it its access for its conditions and its ORDER BY as for a query with a LIMIT (`orderBy.limited` is always true), since only its first row is read: the scan stops at the first row that passes the filter. So a subquery reads and holds no more than the select of its text with `limit 1`, and one that sorts reads what the storage sorts for that select. In goqite's receive, goqite's index `(queue, priority desc, created)` delivers the order of the subquery, and `id = (subquery)` of the statement is then a get by the key, as `id = ?` is. LIMIT 0 reads nothing and gives NULL. A subquery whose value is NULL, because it found no row, has LIMIT 0, or found NULL in its column, makes its comparison never true, so the access of the statement is `none` and reads nothing.
  - **Of several rows, the first is drawn.** Where the ORDER BY of the subquery does not decide which row is first, because it has none or rows tie under it, the value is that of the first row of its access, which reads forward or backward as the engine draws it for a query (`Engine.reading`, `reading.inner`). The engine draws once for each subquery that it reads, after the draw of the statement, which is the direction of a query or of RETURNING. Where an index delivers the order, rows that tie come in the order of the index, as for a select, by the spec. A statement without a subquery draws as before, so the sequence of draws of every seeded test is unchanged. A path that a test forces (`Path`) holds for the subqueries too.
  - **NULL.** A subquery without a row gives NULL, and a comparison with NULL matches no row, for every operator, by the rule of `condition.holds` and `never` that was there.
  - **A correlated subquery is refused,** as `s4ql: not supported: correlated subquery (at offset N)`, N the offset of the name. A name is correlated where it is the column of the select list, of a condition, or of the ORDER BY of the subquery, its table does not have it, and the table of the statement has it. The walk over the names takes such a name as SQLite does, as a column of the table of the statement, without an error, and goes on. The statement is refused once it has given every error that SQLite gives for its names: in a select after the names of the ORDER BY of the statement, and in an update and a delete after the names of RETURNING (`subqueries.uncorrelated`). So s4ql gives SQLite's error wherever SQLite gives one when it prepares the statement, and refuses only what SQLite would run. The refusal names the first correlated name in the order of the walk. A name that neither table has is `no such column`, as in SQLite. A subquery of the table of the statement has no correlated name.
  - **`*` in a subquery.** On a table of one column it is that column. On a table of more columns, in a select, it is SQLite's `row value misused` at the comparison, with the offset of the left side of the comparison, as SQLite gives it. It comes after the tables and the select list, and before the deferred error of a test for NULL of a missing column and the names of the comparison and of the subquery: SQLite expands the `*` of every subquery of a select before it resolves names, and checks the widths of a comparison before its operands. In an update and a delete it is SQLite's `sub-select returns N columns - expected 1` after the names of the WHERE and before RETURNING, or `row value misused` without an offset (-1, as SQLite gives none) for a subquery on the left of `!=` or `<>`. With two or more such subqueries in an update or a delete, which of them SQLite names depends on its plan: `update t set v = 'z' where w = (select * from u3) and k = (select * from u)` names the second, with 2 columns, since SQLite codes the get by the key `k` first. So s4ql refuses such a statement as `s4ql: not supported: second subquery of * of more than one column (at offset N)`, at the second.
  - **The order of the errors,** measured with the pinned shell and pinned by `TestEngine_subquery` against SQLite:
    - **A select.** The table of the statement, then the tables of its subqueries in the order of the text, then the select list, then the walk over the WHERE. At a comparison with a subquery, the walk gives first `row value misused` for `*` of more than one column, then the deferred error of a test for NULL of a missing column, then the names in the order of the text: a subquery on the left before the column, on the right after it. Inside a subquery come its result column, its conditions, with the same deferred error of a test for NULL, and its ORDER BY. Then the ORDER BY of the statement, and then the refusal of a correlated subquery. Then the lengths (`string or blob too big`) of the values of the conditions in the order of the text, those of a subquery, its conditions and then its argument of LIMIT, where it is; then the argument of the LIMIT of the statement. Then the types in the order of the text: for a subquery its own conditions, its argument of LIMIT, and then the type of its column against the column that it is compared with; then the LIMIT of the statement. Then the engine computes the value.
    - **An update and a delete.** The table, the names of SET, and the walk over the WHERE, in which a subquery finds its table when the walk reaches it: `update t set w = 1 where nope = 1 and k = (select k from nope)` gives `no such column: nope`, and `... where k = (select k from nope) returning nope` gives `no such table: nope`. Then `sub-select returns N columns`, then RETURNING, then the refusal of a correlated subquery, then the lengths of SET and of the WHERE, those of a subquery where it is, then the types of the WHERE as for a select, then the value, then the read-only error, and then NOT NULL and the types of SET, as before.
  - **Exact types, by the declared types of the two columns.** The column of a subquery is of a STRICT table, so every value that it gives is of its type or NULL, and SQLite compares it with the column of the statement by the affinities of the two columns, which convert a value only where the two are of other types. So the rule is static, whatever the rows: INT and INTEGER are one type, and every other pair of other types is refused, as `s4ql: not supported: subquery 1, of the column u.k of type INTEGER, compared with the column t.v of type TEXT`. It is refused also where the subquery finds no row or gives NULL. That is stricter than a refusal of a value of another type, but such a refusal would make the answer depend on the rows. Two columns of one storage class give SQLite's rows. The subtest of `TestEngine_subquery` "should give SQLite's rows for every pair of types of a column and of the column of its subquery, or refuse the pair as of other types" runs all 25 pairs of the five types, on both sides, with `=`, `<=`, and `!=` and in an update, on values that SQLite's affinities would make equal across the types (1, 1.0, '1', and x'31'). It requires SQLite's rows for the 7 pairs of one class and the refusal for the 18 others, 72 refusals.
  - **The LIMIT of a subquery follows s4ql's rule of slice 13.** A literal is an integer from 0, as the pass requires, and an argument must be one too, or the statement gives `s4ql: not supported: argument N, which is X, for LIMIT, which takes an integer from 0`. Within that rule the results are SQLite's: 0 gives NULL, and any other count the first row.
  - **Telemetry.** A statement logs one event, as before, with new attributes. `statement.subqueries` is the number of subqueries in the text, on every statement with one, also one that failed. Once the access is chosen, as for `statement.access`, the event also has `statement.subqueries.found`, how many of them found a row; `statement.subqueries.rows_read`, the rows that they read together; `statement.subqueries.access`, the access of each in the words of `statement.access`, in the order of the text, joined by commas, and `none` also for LIMIT 0; `statement.subqueries.index`, the index of each, joined by commas and empty for one without, present only where one has an index; and `statement.subqueries.order`, `delivered` or `sorted` for each, empty for one without ORDER BY, present only where one has an ORDER BY. `statement.rows_read` now counts the rows of the subqueries too, every row that the statement read from the cache. Goqite's receive of a message has `statement.subqueries:1 statement.subqueries.found:1 statement.subqueries.rows_read:1 statement.subqueries.access:index statement.subqueries.index:goqite_queue_priority_created_idx statement.subqueries.order:delivered statement.access:primary_key`. So the question "why was this receive slow or empty" is answered by `found`, which is 0 where no message was ready, and by `rows_read`, `access`, and `order`, which show a sort or a scan.
  - **The commit format does not change,** and neither does replay: the annotation holds the text with the subquery and its arguments.
- **For the builder of the generated checks.** `stmttest.Sequence` still writes no subquery. The engine's `pair.step` now takes a statement with subqueries. Before the call, `pair.subqueriesOf` asks SQLite for the candidates of each subquery: every row of its WHERE that ties with the first under its ORDER BY, or every row without an ORDER BY, and NULL without a row or for LIMIT 0. It also asks for the value of the first row of SQLite's select of the subquery alone. A test hook, `Engine.ObserveSubqueries` of `export_test.go`, observes the values that the engine computes, and can replace them: it sets the unexported `Engine.observe`, which `reading.observe` carries into the build. The values of each instance must be candidates. Where a subquery has more than one candidate, the other instance of the pair uses the values of the first instance in place of its own, and SQLite runs the statement with each subquery replaced by a `?` bound to the value of the instance (`subqueryCall.flatten`). Once the engine has computed values, SQLite and every later comparison read the flattened statement also where each subquery has one candidate, which is equivalent, since that candidate is SQLite's value: the order of ORDER BY and LIMIT, the order of RETURNING, and `everyPath`. The rule of types for a statement with a subquery is `subqueryRule`: the correlated refusal, two wide `*`, the types in the order of the text, the LIMIT of the subquery, and then the LIMIT or the SET of the statement. The pair counts four kinds under "reached: ": "subquery whose value was NULL", "subquery whose value was that of its one first row", "subquery with rows that tie for the first, whose value was that of SQLite's first", and "subquery with rows that tie for the first, whose value was that of another". `TestEngine_subquery` requires the last two, by the spec's rule that an unspecified order varies. Still skipped for a statement with a subquery: `pair.reach`, the kinds of what a statement reaches, and the rule of a literal longer than the length limit, `pair.checkLength`, which does not read the literals of a subquery. `reachChange` of the driver check, `stmttest.SelectOf` and `stmttest.WhereCases`, and `orderedOf` of the engine's tests still drop subqueries; the pair flattens the statement before it uses them. `engine.AccessOf` and `PathAccessOf` of `export_test.go` are not given a subquery. The open item is in [the generated workload](generated-workload.md).

## Review of the engine step

- **What an independent review of the engine step found, closed by a builder on 2026-11-24.** The review found no wrong result in the engine; it found a crash of the pair and planted faults that every test passed. Each fault below was planted again, and a test of `internal/engine` now fails on it.
  - **A select whose subqueries the engine does not compute** (LIMIT 0 of the statement, as a literal or an argument, or another condition that is never true) made `pair.step` panic in `orderedOf`, since such a statement is not flattened. The pair now requires such a select to give no row, compares its rows with SQLite's of the text, skips the comparisons of order and access, and counts it as "subquery that the engine did not compute, of a select that gave no row", which `TestEngine_subquery` requires, only through Query, where its rows are compared. An update or a delete whose subqueries the engine does not compute counts as "subquery that the engine did not compute, of an update or a delete", required too, and the count of changes and the state compare it with SQLite's run of its text; a planted fault that computes them for a condition that is never true fails the requirement. A planted fault that computes no subquery for a statement whose only condition has one fails it.
  - **A subquery that takes the first row whose value is not NULL:** cases where NULL comes first under the ORDER BY and later rows have values, in a select, an update, and a delete.
  - **A subquery that reads under `context.Background()`:** a subtest ends the context at each of its checks in turn while the subquery reads 40 rows at least, with the count of checks of `TestEngine_cancelChange`, for a select, an update, and a delete: each run fails with the error of the context and changes nothing, and the statement is canceled at more than 40 points. A subquery that sorts is not in it: the storage sorts and looks at the context only every so many steps, so the count would not tell the plant apart without depending on its numbers.
  - **`statement.subqueries.found` of a subquery that reads rows and finds none,** as a receive of goqite where no message is due: three cases in the subtest of the event of `TestEngine_report`.
  - **`*` of more than one column with `!=` and `<>` on the right in an update and a delete,** which is `sub-select returns 3 columns - expected 1`, and with `=` and `<` on the left; and in a select with `!=` and `<>`.

## Stricter than SQLite or different from it, for the lead to tell Markus

- A subquery of two or more named columns is not supported, where SQLite gives `row value misused`, or `no such table` first: an error either way.
- A subquery whose WHERE is deeper than SQLite resolves is not supported, where SQLite gives `Expression tree is too large` once it has found the tables, and `no such table` on an empty database.
- `v is (select ...)` is now `IS with a value other than NULL` where it was `subquery`, and a text whose first refused token is inside a subquery gets the error for that token where it got `subquery`; both are refused, as before.
- Every form that SQLite takes and the slice does not name stays refused, as the brief listed: IN, EXISTS, a subquery outside a comparison of a WHERE, a nested subquery, a compound, and a subquery after a sign.
- A correlated subquery is not supported, where SQLite runs it and takes the name from the table of the statement. s4ql first gives SQLite's error wherever SQLite has one for the names.
- A subquery whose column is of another type than the column that it is compared with is not supported, by the declared types, with INT and INTEGER as one type, where SQLite compares by affinity. This holds also where the subquery finds no row or gives NULL.
- An argument of `limit ?` in a subquery must be an integer from 0, where SQLite reads the LIMIT X of a scalar subquery as X <> 0: with `'x'`, -1, 1.5, or `x'00'` SQLite gives the first row, and with NULL it gives `datatype mismatch` only where it needs the value.
- s4ql always computes a subquery, once, before the statement, and makes every check of it first, so a refusal of the subquery, for its types or its LIMIT, shows also where SQLite's plan would never compute it. For example, SQLite gives no error for `limit ?` bound to NULL on an empty table, or where a get by the key finds nothing first. With the supported SQL, a subquery has no error at run time of its own, only those of the storage and of the context.
- Two subqueries of `*` of more than one column in an update or a delete are not supported, at the second, where SQLite gives `sub-select returns N columns - expected 1` for the one that its plan codes first. With one such subquery, s4ql gives SQLite's error.
- Where the ORDER BY of a subquery does not decide its first row, s4ql gives any row that a legal order puts first, drawn for each run, where SQLite gives the first of its plan.
- The offsets of errors are not promised. `row value misused` in a select has SQLite's offset, that of a column on the left of the comparison, and none (-1) for a subquery on the left, and the errors of SQLite's code generator in an update or a delete have none (-1), as in SQLite.
