# Slice 12: a value on the left of a comparison

The decisions and notes of the builders of [slice 12 of the roadmap](../roadmap.md#slice-12-a-value-on-the-left-of-a-comparison).

## The statement pass

- **Decisions of the statement pass of slice 12, a value on the left of a comparison, by its builder on 2026-11-21, built, for the lead to confirm.** Each fact of SQLite here was found with the pinned shell or the binding, and the tests of the pass check it against SQLite: `checkWhere` and `checkChange` on every supported text, and the subtests "should read a value on the left as SQLite does" and "should give SQLite's errors for a value on the left in the order of the other side" of `TestCheck`. The README of the pass has the forms token for token.
  - A condition is normalised at recognition: `5 < a` is `stmt.Condition{Column: a, Operator: OpGreater, Value: 5, ValueFirst: true}`. Every condition reads column, operator, value, so the engine's fitting of conditions to the key and to indexes, its filter, its rule of exact types, and the rules of `stmttest` run it unchanged, and one that ignores `ValueFirst` still gives the right rows. Kept as written, with the operator as the text has it, each of them would have to turn it round, and one that forgot would give wrong rows without an error. The text's order is kept in `ValueFirst`, `Column.Offset`, and `Value.Span`, and `stmt.Operator.Swapped` gives the operator as written: `<` and `>`, `<=` and `>=` swap, `=` and `!=` stay, a test for NULL has none. A plan and a golden file show the condition with its column first.
  - Parameters are numbered in the order of the text, whichever side they are on: `a = ? and ? < b and c > ?` numbers them 1, 2, and 3, as SQLite does.
  - A value on the left is what slice 7 refused as `value on the left of a comparison`: a literal or a `?`, with at most one sign. It is read by the code that reads the value on the right, and every form that the right side refuses is refused on the left with the same name and at the same token: `+ sign`, `hex literal`, `TRUE or FALSE`, `parameter after a sign` (at the `?`), `NULL after a sign`, `string after a sign`, `blob after a sign`, `number with a digit separator`, `current date or time`, `infinite real number`, `integer outside 64 bits`, `numbered parameter`, and `named parameter`. `- -5 = v`, `~1 < v`, and `-v = 1` keep `unary operator` at the start, as before slice 12. A literal NULL on the left is supported: `null = a` gives no rows, as `a = null` does.
  - New names of "not supported": `value compared with a value`, for a literal or a `?` on both sides (`1 = 1`, `? = ?`, `1 = ?`, `'v' = 1`), at the value on the right, as `column compared with a column` is at the column on the right; and `value before IS`, for a literal or a `?` before IS (`5 is null`, `null is null`, `? is null`, `5 is not null`, `5 is v`, `5 is distinct from v`), at the value. The name `value on the left of a comparison` is no longer given by the pass. `5 isnull` stays `NULL test`.
  - The column on the right is read as the column on the left is: a name in double quotes is a column (the build has double-quoted strings off), a keyword that SQLite reads as a column on the left is one on the right too (the same 84), and `5 < t.v` is `column with the name of a table`, `5 < true` `TRUE or FALSE`, `5 < (v)` `parentheses`, `5 < [v]` `name in brackets`, all at the column. A column on both sides stays `column compared with a column`.
  - The depth of a condition with a sign before its value is 3 on either side, so a WHERE of `-1 = v` and 997 more conditions is the deepest that SQLite takes, as for `v = -1`.
  - `stmttest.Generator.ValueFirst`, 0 by default: one comparison in ValueFirst, at random or from a hook of `Generator.Where`, `Generator.Update`, or `Generator.Delete`, is written with its value first, the operator swapped in any spelling, and its column on the right, and the condition that the generator gives says `ValueFirst`; a hook's comparison with `ValueFirst` set is written so whatever ValueFirst is. With ValueFirst 0 and no such comparison, the generator's output is byte for byte what it was. The pass's own tests set ValueFirst to 3; the engine's and the driver's leave it at 0, and `Sequence` writes none.
  - Until the engine ran slice 12, `engine.valueFirstRefusal`, called in `engine.check`, gave (it is gone since the engine step below; this is the record of the pass) a statement with a value on the left the error that the pass gave it before: `s4ql: not supported: value on the left of a comparison`, at the start of the value of the first such condition. A text with a value on the left and a later token that the pass refuses gets the pass's error for that token, where before it got the one for the value: `1 = v or w = 2` gives `OR`, `1 = 1` gives `value compared with a value`, and a form of the value that the pass refuses gets its own name, so `+1 < v` gives `+ sign`, `0x1 = v` `hex literal`, and `null is null` `value before IS`. The name `value on the left of a comparison` is no longer in the vocabulary of the README of the pass, since the pass gives it no more. The driver's `Prepare` calls `stmt.Check` alone, so it takes such a text, and its first run gives the refusal.
- **Facts of SQLite for the builder of the engine of slice 12, measured by the builder of its statement pass on 2026-11-21.** Pinned by the subtests of `TestCheck` named above, unless it says otherwise.
  - `value op column` gives the rows of `column op' value`, with op' the operator that `Swapped` gives, for every operator in every spelling and 20 values of every type, NULL and the limits of 64 bits included, as a literal and as a `?`, on a strict table with a column of each type, INT, REAL, TEXT, BLOB, and ANY, and on a table that is not strict: 2,880 selects. SQLite applies the affinity of the column to the value on either side, so the two orders agree also where it converts the value, such as `'5' = a` on an INT column of a table that is not strict.
  - The rule of exact types therefore treats both orders alike: `'5' < a` on an INT column is refused as `a > '5'` is. The refusal names the value and the column, `a string literal compared with the column t1.a of type INT`, which reads true for either order, so the engine can give the same message.
  - SQLite uses the key and an index for a value on the left as for the other order: `EXPLAIN QUERY PLAN` gives `SEARCH t USING PRIMARY KEY (k>?)` for `5 < k`, `(k=?)` for `5 = k` and `null = k`, and `SEARCH t USING INDEX i (a>? AND a<?)` for `5 >= a and a > 1` (measured with the shell only).
  - The order of the errors is that of slice 7, with the names in the order of the text: on a table that has neither column, `'<1,200 bytes>' < a and nope = 1` and `'x' < a and 1 = nope` give `no such column: nope` under the length limit of 1,000, so a missing column of a later condition comes before a value on the left that is too long or of another type. `nope is null and nope2 is null and 1 = nope3` names `nope2`, and `nope is null and 1 = nope2` names `nope`, as with the column on the left. `select nope from t where '<long>' < a` names `nope`, and `select * from nope where ...` gives `no such table: nope`. After the names comes `string or blob too big`: `'x' < a and '<long>' = b` gives it, and SQLite never refuses a value for its type, so the engine's refusal of a value of another type than its column comes last, for the first such condition in the order of the text, as it does for the other order. A missing column in double quotes on the right, `5 < "nope"`, gives `no such column: "nope" - should this be a string literal in single-quotes?`, as on the left.

## The engine and the driver

- **Decisions of the engine step of slice 12, a value on the left of a comparison, by its builder on 2026-11-21, built, for the lead to confirm.** The agreement with Markus of 2026-11-20: in a WHERE, a comparison may have the literal or `?` on the left and the column on the right, in a select, an update, and a delete; it means what SQLite says; the rule of exact types holds as for the other order; the key and indexes are used as in slices 7 and 9. The tests of each point are against SQLite.
  - `engine.valueFirstRefusal` is gone, and the engine runs the form. The plan's `engine.Condition` and the engine's `condition` carry `ValueFirst` from the statement, with the operator turned round as the pass gives it, and nothing in the engine reads it but the planted bug `unturnedBound`; `engine.Format` writes the condition with its column first, as the pass decided. The driver's `Prepare` took such a text already; its first run now runs it.
  - No message of the engine needed the text's order. `no such column` has the offset of the column as the text has it, which is SQLite's, also for a column on the right; the refusal by type names the value and the column, `a string literal compared with the column t.a of type INT`, which reads rightly in either order; the number of an argument is its number in the order of the text; and the refusals of the arguments, `wrong number of arguments` and an argument that no column holds, are made before the database, as for the other order. `TestEngine_valueFirst` in `internal/engine/valuefirst_test.go` and `TestValueFirst` in `conn_test.go` pin each.
  - The order of the errors, as measured: the names first in the order of the text, also after a value on the left that is too long; then `string or blob too big`; then the refusal by type of the first value of another type in the order of the text, whichever side it is on. A literal that is too long in the WHERE of an update or a delete is the known difference of a long literal of [slice 8](slice-08.md) on either side: SQLite gives `string or blob too big` there only when it steps the statement, and resets the count of changes, where s4ql gives the error before and keeps the count; the generated checks excuse it as before (`excusedLongChange`).
  - The access is the same for both orders: `TestChooseAccess` checks every case through the statement with its comparisons as written, all turned round, and every second one turned; the golden plans have goqite's receive as goqite writes it, `queue = ? and ? >= timeout and received < ?`, beside the one with `timeout <= ?`, and `TestAccessOf_golden` requires the two to be equal; `TestEngine_rowsRead` reads both with the same 3000, 1000, and 500 rows.
  - `stmttest.Sequence` writes one comparison in three with its value first (`Generator.ValueFirst` 3), chosen as the rate of the four tried (2 to 5) that took the fewest least numbers of `TestDifferential` below their numbers. `stmttest.whereText` writes a condition with `ValueFirst` with its `?` on the left, so that the paths that `pair.everyPath` forces, and SQLite's side of the checks of an update, run the form of the text. `stmttest.ValueFirstCases` names the comparisons with a value on the left, by their operator as the text writes it, and those with NULL; the engine's generated checks and the driver check count them by outcome, "finds rows", "finds no row", or each refusal, and require each.
  - The planted bug `unturnedBound` is in `fit` in `internal/engine/access.go`: a condition with its value on the left bounds its column by the operator as the text writes it, so `5 < a` reads the rows below 5. It is there because `fit` is the one place where the direction of an operator decides which end of a scan a value bounds, and a fault there shows only as missing rows, which the filter cannot give back; the regression it stands for is a reader who turns a condition round that the pass has turned round already. The simulation finds it in the hammer workload, 33 of the first 200 seeds, 26 by rows that differ from SQLite's; `TestDifferential_plantedBugs` in 3 of 60 sequences.
