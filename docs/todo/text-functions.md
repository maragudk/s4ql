# Text functions

The decisions and notes of the builders of the piece of work "text functions", the first step into text in expressions after [arithmetic](arithmetic.md): the operator `||` and the functions `lower` and `hex`, in `set` and in `where`. The id of goqite's queue, `id text primary key default ('m_' || lower(hex(randomblob(16))))`, is the milestone, not the specification. `randomblob`, `strftime`, and `default` of an expression are later pieces of work.

## The statement pass

- **Decisions of the statement pass of text functions, by its builder on 2026-11-26, built, for the lead to confirm.** Each fact of SQLite here was measured with the pinned shell (3.54.1), and the tests of the pass check the tree and the literals against SQLite, as for arithmetic: `checkExpressions` in `internal/sql/stmt/stmt_test.go` on every supported text, and the cases of `TestCheck` under "Text in expressions" and "Text that is not supported". [The section Text of the README of the pass](../../internal/sql/stmt/README.md#text) has the forms token for token.
  - **The forms and where.** `operand || operand`, `lower ( operand )`, and `hex ( operand )`, where an operand is a string literal (for `hex` a blob literal too), a plain `?`, a column, a concatenation, or a call. They stand exactly where arithmetic stands: as the value of an assignment of SET, and as either side of a comparison of the WHERE of a select, an update, a delete, and a scalar subquery. Nowhere else: the select list and RETURNING (`result column other than a column or *`), VALUES (`function call` and `binary operator`), IS (`function call`), ORDER BY, LIMIT, and DEFAULT keep their refusals.
  - **The typed form, the contract for the engine.** `stmt.Expression` has two new kinds, `ExprConcat` with two operands and `ExprCall` with one, and a new field `Function`, `FuncLower` or `FuncHex`, which is 0 for every other kind. The `Value` of an `ExprValue` is now a `stmt.Text` as an operand of `||` or the argument of a function, or a `stmt.Blob` as the argument of `hex`, and never `stmt.Null`. The `Span` of a call runs from its name to its closing parenthesis. The parameters are numbered in the order of the text, as before. The root of an expression is the only place where a text node can stand under or beside arithmetic: an operand of arithmetic is never text, and an operand of `||` or an argument is never arithmetic, which `checkPlace` in the tests checks on every statement. What a column or a parameter holds is for the engine.
  - **Refused from the text, each at the token named, with what SQLite does:**
    - `function other than lower or hex`, at the name: `abs(v)`, `upper(v)`, and `randomblob(16)`.
    - `wrong number of arguments`, at the closing parenthesis of `lower()` or the comma of `lower(v, w)`, and `* as an argument` for `hex(*)`. SQLite gives `wrong number of arguments to function lower()` once it has found the tables, and `no such table` first on an empty database, so the pass cannot give its error.
    - `DISTINCT or ALL` for `lower(distinct v)` and `hex(all v)`, which SQLite takes and which change nothing.
    - `ORDER BY clause`, `FILTER clause`, and `OVER clause` for `lower(v order by v)`, `lower(v) filter (where 1)`, and `hex(v) over ()`. SQLite gives `ORDER BY may not be used with non-aggregate lower()`, `FILTER may not be used with non-aggregate lower()`, and `hex() may not be used as a window function` once it has found the tables.
    - `name in brackets` and `name in backticks` for `[lower](v)` and `` `hex`(v) ``, which SQLite takes. A name in double quotes is taken, as SQLite takes it.
    - `text in arithmetic` for a concatenation or a call as an operand of `+`, `-`, `*`, `/`, or a negation: `v * lower(w)`, `-hex(w)`, `(w || 'a') * 2`, and `v + 1 || 'a'`. SQLite reads the text as a number.
    - `number in a concatenation` for an integer or real literal, a negative number, an operator of arithmetic, or a negation as an operand of `||`: `w || 1`, `1.5 || w`, `w || -1`, `w || (w + 1)`, `w || -w`, and `-? || w`. SQLite writes `1 || 2` as the text `12`, a compatibility quirk that s4ql does not copy, so only text meets `||`.
    - `NULL in a concatenation` for `w || null`, which SQLite gives as NULL, and `blob in a concatenation` for `x'01' || w`, which SQLite reads as text.
    - `number as an argument`, `NULL as an argument`, and `blob as an argument` (`hex` takes a blob) for `lower(1)`, `hex(-1)`, `hex(w + 1)`, `lower(-w)`, `hex(null)`, and `lower(x'41')`. SQLite writes a number as text, gives NULL for `lower(null)`, gives `''` for `hex(null)`, and reads a blob as text.
    - `parentheses` around the argument of a call, `lower((w))` and `hex((w || 'a'))`, and around a call, `(lower(w))` and `'a' || (hex(w))`: they hold nothing of their own, as for arithmetic.
    - The names of a value keep their meaning: `lower(-'a')` is `string after a sign`, `w || current_time` is `current date or time`, `lower(true)` is `TRUE or FALSE`, `hex(t.w)` is `column with the name of a table`, `w || ?1` is `numbered parameter`, `'a' || (w % 2)` is `binary operator`, and a COLLATE and a subquery keep `COLLATE` and `subquery`.
    - A concatenation or a call without a column is a value, so `'a' || 'b' = 'ab'` and `? = lower(?)` are `value compared with a value`. Syntax errors are SQLite's: `lower(w` and `w ||` are `incomplete input`, `lower(w,)` is `near ")": syntax error`, and `main.lower(w)` is `near "(": syntax error`.
  - **Names that changed.** In SET and in a comparison, a function was `function call` and is `function other than lower or hex` now, and `lower(v) = 'a'` was `function call` and is supported. `v || 'a'` was `binary operator` and is supported, and `v + (w || 'a')` and `v + abs(v)` were `binary operator` and `function call`, and are `text in arithmetic` and `function other than lower or hex`. In VALUES, after IS, and in the select list the old names stay.
  - **The depth and the parentheses.** `||` is an operator, one level more than its deeper operand, and a call is one level more than its argument. `TestCheck` checks chains of `||`, also inside a call, at both edges against SQLite. The parenthesis of a call counts toward the limit of 100 open parentheses; `a || lower(` repeated 100 times is supported and 101 times is `parentheses deeper than 100`, and SQLite's parser takes both. The subtest of the parentheses leaves out `hex`, whose text is twice as long as its argument at each level, so that SQLite cannot hold 100 levels of it.
- **Where s4ql is stricter than SQLite or differs from it, in the statement pass,** for the lead to tell Markus:
  - A number, NULL, or blob literal, or arithmetic, meeting `||` or a function is refused. SQLite writes the number as text, gives NULL for NULL, and reads the blob as text.
  - Text from `||` or a function in arithmetic is refused. SQLite reads it as a number.
  - `DISTINCT` and `ALL` in a call are refused, where SQLite takes both.
  - A name of a function in brackets or in backticks is refused, where SQLite takes it.
  - Parentheses around the argument of a call or around a call are refused; SQLite reads them as what they hold.
  - Another number of arguments, `*`, `ORDER BY`, `FILTER`, and `OVER` in a call are "not supported" from the text. SQLite refuses them only once it has found the tables, with its own message.
  - A name of a function in double quotes is taken, as SQLite takes it.
- **What the evaluator and the engine need to know.** These facts of SQLite were measured with the pinned shell, and the tests of the pass do not check them all.
  - **`lower`** changes only the ASCII letters: `lower('ÀBC')` is `'Àbc'` and `lower('É')` is `'É'`. `lower(NULL)` is NULL.
  - **`hex`** gives the bytes in upper-case hexadecimal digits, of a text its UTF-8 bytes: `hex('é')` is `'C3A9'`. `hex(x'')` and `hex('')` are `''`, and `hex(NULL)` is `''`, so a NULL in gives a text out and not NULL.
  - **`||`** gives NULL for `NULL || 'a'` and `'a' || NULL`. The result of each of the three is text.
  - **Conversion.** SQLite converts a number or a blob from a column or an argument as it does for a literal: `k || 'a'` is `'1a'` for `k = 1`, `lower(k)` is `'1'`, `hex(k)` is `'31'`, and `'a' || x'4243'` is `'aBC'`. The engine must refuse these by the types of the columns and the arguments, which the pass does not know.
  - **Storing.** `update t set k = lower(v)` into an INT column gives `cannot store TEXT value in INT column t.k`.
  - **The engine refused text until its own step,** which removed the refusal; see [The engine and the driver](#the-engine-and-the-driver).
  - **SQLite looks the names inside a call up in an order of its own,** found by the review of this step: `resolveExprStep` ignores the error of the arguments of a function (`case TK_FUNCTION` in `internal/sqlite/c/sqlite3.c`) and goes on, and a later error replaces the message, until the walk reaches an operator. So `delete from t where hex(nosuch) = nosuch2` gives `no such column: nosuch2`, `update t set s = lower(nosuch1) || nosuch2` gives `nosuch2`, `select * from t where hex(nosuch) || s || x = 'a'` gives `no such column: x`, and `delete from t where lower(nosuch1) <> (select a from nosuch2)` gives `no such table: nosuch2`, while `lower(nosuch) = s and nosuch2 = 1` gives `nosuch`. The engine step copies this order; see [The engine and the driver](#the-engine-and-the-driver).
  - **The types that the engine must decide.** A TEXT column, a text argument, and NULL from a column or an argument meet `||` and `lower`; `hex` also takes a BLOB. An INT, INTEGER, or REAL column, and an argument that is a number, must be refused, where SQLite converts. The result is text, so it compares with and is assigned to a TEXT column only. `hex(NULL)` is `''` and not NULL. The result of `hex` is twice as long as its argument, so it can pass the length limit of a text.
- **Decided after the review of the statement pass, by its builder on 2026-11-26:**
  - The place of an operand is refused before the operand is read, as `binary operator` is for arithmetic: `lower(1) + 2` and `'a' || 1 + 2` are `text in arithmetic`, at the first token of the operand, and not the refusal of what the operand holds. A concatenation is text whatever its operands, so `abs(w) || 'a' + 1` is `text in arithmetic` too.
  - A plain value compared with text is left to the engine, as for arithmetic: `lower(v) = 1`, `v || 'a' = 1.5`, `lower(v) = x'00'`, `lower(v) = null`, `lower(v) = ?`, and `lower(v) = n + 1` are supported by the pass. SQLite compares by its order of types, `lower(v) > 1` is true for every row whose `v` is not NULL, so the engine must refuse a literal, an argument, a column, or an arithmetic expression of another class than text on the other side of a text expression, as the agreed rule refuses a comparison of values of different types.
  - `leftmostOffset` in `internal/engine/expression.go` stops at a call, whose name is the offset of SQLite's `row value misused` for a subquery of `*` on the right: `lower(v) = (select * from u)` is at the `lower`, `   LOWER (v) != (select * from u)` at the `LOWER`. `TestLeftmostOffset` pins the offsets that SQLite gives; the engine reaches it for text once it no longer refuses text first.
  - `stmttest.Quirk` read two operands of every node that is not a negation, which a call does not have; the engine step gives a concatenation and a call no quirk.
- **Open:** the generators of `stmttest` do not write text yet, which is the step of the generated checks. Mutation testing of the new code of the pass is open too.

## The evaluator

- **Decisions of the evaluator of text functions, by its builder on 2026-11-26, built, for the lead to confirm.** Each fact of SQLite here was measured through the binding and the pinned shell (3.54.1) and is pinned in `TestBound_Eval_text`, `TestCheck`, and `TestProgram_Type` of `internal/expr/expr_test.go`, with SQLite's answer and s4ql's side by side. [The README of the package](../../internal/expr/README.md) has the API and the rules in full.
  - **The types.** `expr.Type` gains `TypeText` and `TypeBlob`. `||` and `lower` take text and give text; `hex` takes text or a blob and gives text. `hex` of text is taken: SQLite's answer is the hex of its UTF-8 bytes, which converts nothing. A column alone has the type of its column, so a TEXT column alone is `TypeText` and a BLOB column alone `TypeBlob`, where `Check` refused both before; the engine copies a column alone without the evaluator, so no path of the engine reaches this. A tree has no arithmetic under text and no text under arithmetic, as the pass gives it, so "mixed with arithmetic" means two trees, such as the two sides of a comparison, which is the engine's to judge.
  - **What `Check` and `Bind` refuse**, as a `*stmt.NotSupportedError` at the offset of the column or the `?`, the first in the order of the text, columns before arguments: in `||`, a column of type INT, INTEGER, REAL, or BLOB (`column of type INT in a concatenation`) and an argument that is an integer, a real, or a blob (`argument 2 in a concatenation, which is an integer`); the same for the argument of `lower` (`column of type BLOB in a call of lower`, `argument 1 in a call of lower, which is a real`); for the argument of `hex`, a column of type INT, INTEGER, or REAL and an argument that is a number (`column of type INTEGER in a call of hex`, `argument 1 in a call of hex, which is a real`); and in arithmetic, as before. SQLite converts each: `a || 'x'` is `'1x'`, `lower(x)` reads a blob as text, and `hex(1.5)` is `'312E35'`. A literal of another type than its place, an operator of arithmetic under text or the reverse, and a function other than `lower` and `hex`, which the pass never gives, are internal errors, in `Check` and again in `Eval`.
  - **NULL**, from a column or an argument, as SQLite gives it: `NULL || 'a'` and `'a' || NULL` are NULL, `lower(NULL)` is NULL, and `hex(NULL)` is `''`, also through `hex(lower(NULL))`. So an argument that is NULL makes `||` and `lower` of the type `TypeNull`, and leaves `hex` `TypeText`. Both operands of `||` are computed, and an error of one comes beside a NULL in the other; SQLite computes both too, so it fails the same way.
  - **The values.** `||` joins the bytes, the left first. `lower` changes the bytes `A` to `Z` only, as SQLite does without ICU: `lower('ÀBCÉé')` is `'ÀbcÉé'`, and the Kelvin sign and the dotted capital I, which Unicode folds, stay. `hex` writes upper-case digits. Each treats a text as its bytes, as SQLite does, so it gives SQLite's answer also for a text that is not valid UTF-8, which `TestBound_Eval_text` pins for each of the three. The engine refuses such an argument before it binds it (`checkArgument` in `internal/engine/errors.go`), and s4ql stores none, so the evaluator never meets one there; every text that the three make of valid UTF-8 without NUL is valid UTF-8 without NUL.
  - **The length limit and the new error.** `Program.Bind` takes the length limit as its second argument: `Bind(args []*s4qlpb.Value, limit int)`. It takes a limit below 30 as 30 and one above 1,000,000,000 as 1,000,000,000, as SQLite does (`SQLITE_MIN_LENGTH` and `SQLITE_MAX_LENGTH`). SQLite gives `string or blob too big` where it would allocate more than the limit, and counts one byte more for `lower` and `hex` than for `||`, for the NUL at the end (`OP_Concat` and `contextMalloc` in `internal/sqlite/c/sqlite3.c`): `||` fails for a result longer than the limit, `lower` for a text as long as the limit or longer, and `hex` where twice the length of its argument plus one is longer than the limit. With a limit of 1,000, `s || t` of 500 and 500 bytes is a text of 1,000 bytes, of 500 and 501 bytes it fails, `lower` of 999 bytes is a text and of 1,000 fails, and `hex` of 499 bytes is a text and of 500 fails; with a limit of 1,001, `hex` of 500 bytes is a text. The evaluator gives `expr.ErrTooLong` there, an `*expr.Error` that matches `expr.ErrEvaluation`, with the message `s4ql: text or blob longer than the length limit in hex (at offset 17)`. `expr.Error` has a new field `Function`, the function of a failed call, whose name the message gives in place of the operator. No other error of evaluation comes from the three: they have no overflow, no division, and nothing that fails on bytes.
  - **A literal longer than the limit**, found by the review: SQLite fails with `string or blob too big` for a text literal longer than the limit where it computes it, also beside NULL, as in `s || '<31 bytes>'` with `s` NULL under a limit of 30, and reads a blob literal longer than the limit as NULL, so that `hex(x'<31 bytes>')` is `''`. `Bind` gives `ErrTooLong` for the first such literal in the order of the text, at the literal (`Op` is `stmt.ExprValue`, and the message says `in a literal`), after its refusals of types and before anything is computed. For the blob, s4ql's error is stricter than SQLite's NULL.
  - **What a statement longer than the limit does**, corrected by the review: SQLite runs a statement longer than the limit (a 73-byte update under `.limit length 30`). What fails is a literal longer than the limit, above, and a result column without a name whose text, its name, is longer than the limit (`select 2+1+... from t` fails where `... as a` runs). The oracle of the tests names its column, so it has no need to raise a case's limit.
  - **Allocations.** Arithmetic still allocates nothing (`TestBound_Eval_allocations`). An operation of text allocates its result once, and nothing where its result is one of its operands or empty: `||` with the empty text, `lower` of a text without an ASCII capital letter, and `hex` of the empty text, the empty blob, or NULL. `'m_' || lower(hex(x))` costs two allocations, or three where the digits have a letter `A` to `F`. A text is not copied from the row, the arguments, or a literal, and `hex` reads a blob where it is; a BLOB column alone copies its bytes. `expr.Value` grew from 16 to 32 bytes, for a string; the benchmark of arithmetic could not be compared with `main` on 2026-11-26, since the machine's load was 27 and both took 220 to 870 ns per run. A caller that evaluates a row at a time may keep the value, and its `Proto`, after the row is gone: Go's strings do not change, so a value that shares its bytes with a row stays as it was.
- **Where s4ql is stricter than SQLite or differs from it, in the evaluator,** for the lead to tell Markus:
  - A number from a column or an argument in `||`, `lower`, or `hex`, and a blob in `||` or `lower`, are refused; SQLite converts them to text. The pass refuses the literals already.
  - A blob literal longer than the length limit is an error, where SQLite reads it as NULL.
  - Nowhere does s4ql give another value than SQLite for one row. A text over the limit is an error in both. When the error comes is another matter, for the engine step: see below.
- **The check against SQLite.** `TestDifferential` and `FuzzExpr` in `internal/expr/differential_test.go` check text as they check arithmetic, with the row of the columns `a INT`, `b INTEGER`, `c REAL`, `d REAL`, `s TEXT`, `x BLOB`, `t TEXT`, and `y BLOB`, and SQLite under the length limit of the case. `reference` computes text apart from the evaluator, with `unicode.ToLower` on the ASCII bytes and `hex.EncodeToString`, and SQLite must give its value; for `expr.ErrTooLong` of an operation, SQLite must fail with `string or blob too big` for the operation alone and for the whole expression, and for one of a literal, SQLite must fail for a text literal alone and give NULL for a blob literal alone. A third of the cases of the generator are text: trees of 1 to 5 levels of `||`, `lower`, and `hex` over literals, columns, and parameters, with empty text, every kind of ASCII byte, letters that are not ASCII, blobs as the argument of `hex`, and NULL one leaf in ten; one in four has a limit from 1,000 to 4,000 bytes, most of those with lengths at the edge of the limit, a NULL beside the operation at the edge one time in four, and a literal longer than the limit one leaf in 30; one in 30 is a chain of more than 900 `||` or calls nested 90 to 100 deep; one in 25 has a number or a blob where text is due. Each seed has 3,600 cases, from 2,500, so that arithmetic keeps about as many. The 28,800 cases take about 5 s. The counts of `testdata/reached/TestDifferential.kinds` were derived again at a third of the totals: of text, 7,036 values of text, 683 texts too long (320 of `||`, 165 of `lower`, 198 of `hex`, 164 of them beside NULL), 286 of `||`, 243 of `lower`, and 108 of `hex` at the limit, 127 text literals and 9 blob literals longer than the limit, 138 cases deeper than 900, and refusals of most places. A case has one planted refusal, so the refusals of an argument of `lower` or `hex` and of a column of type INTEGER in a call of `lower` are too rare to require, and the order of two refusals in one case is pinned in `TestCheck` only.
  - **The planted faults**, each by hand in `internal/expr/expr.go`, each found by `TestDifferential` (10 failures, where it stops) and by the seeds of `FuzzExpr`, and reverted: `lower` that folds letters that are not ASCII; `hex` in lower case; `||` with its operands swapped; `hex(NULL)` NULL; a NULL operand of `||` that gives the other operand; the limit of `||` one byte too low; the limit of `lower` one byte too high; the limit of `hex` one byte too high. These are 8 faults. They were planted before the fixes of the review, which change none of the lines that they touch.
  - **`FuzzExpr`** takes the text, the row, the arguments, and a limit (0 for SQLite's default, otherwise from 30 to 4,000 bytes), and encodes a text or a blob by its length and its bytes; a text is made valid UTF-8 without NUL, and an input with more than eight calls of `hex` is skipped, since nine double a text to gigabytes. Its seeds are the generator's cases but the deep trees, and 47 inputs of text from a run of five minutes on 2026-11-26 (5.2 million executions, 980 inputs of its corpus, no failure), in `internal/expr/testdata/fuzz/FuzzExpr`.
- **What the engine step needed to know** was built by it; see [The engine and the driver](#the-engine-and-the-driver). One fact of SQLite stays here: **SQLite computes a constant part of an expression before any row**, found by the review: on an empty table under a limit of 100, with two arguments of 60 bytes, `update t set s = ?1 || ?2 || s`, `delete from t where ?1 || ?2 || s = 'x'`, and `select 1 as a from t where u = 'k' and s = ?1 || ?2` fail with `string or blob too big`, while `update t set s = ?1 || ?2` and `... = lower(?1 || ?2)` do not.
- **Open:** mutation testing of the new code, a long run of `FuzzExpr` under the address sanitizer, which CI does, and the equivalent mutants of `internal/expr` in `internal/sql/mutation/equivalent.txt`, of which this step removed two that no longer name a mutant, renamed two, and added one for `lower`, and whose other reasons the mutation step must check again for the new code.


## The engine and the driver

- **Decisions of the engine step of text functions, by its builder on 2026-11-26, built, for the lead to confirm.** Each fact of SQLite here was measured with the pinned shell (3.54.1), mostly under `.limit length 100` with arguments of 60 bytes, and the tests compare the engine with SQLite under a limit of 1,000 with arguments of 600 bytes: `TestEngine_text` in `internal/engine/expression_test.go` (through the duo of the engine and SQLite, which runs each statement on both and compares the answer, the count of changes, and the tables), `TestText` in `conn_test.go`, and the hand sequence "text in expressions" of `internal/sqlite/crosscheck/drivercheck`.
  - **Where text runs.** `||`, `lower`, and `hex` run in the `set` of an update and on either side of a comparison of the WHERE of a select, an update, a delete, and a scalar subquery, in every statement of `ExecAll`, and through the driver's `Prepare`, `Exec`, and `Query`. `textRefusal` and `engine.Check`'s refusal are gone. The plan is the `*Arithmetic` of arithmetic, which holds any `stmt.Expression`.
  - **The types.** A text expression, a concatenation or a call at its root, is of the class text, also where an argument is NULL and every value is NULL (`expressionClass`), so it meets a TEXT column, a string literal, a text argument, NULL, a subquery of a TEXT column, and another text expression, and nothing else. SQLite orders a number before text, so `lower(v) > 1` is true for every row whose `v` is not NULL, a quirk that s4ql refuses: `lower(s) = 1` is `an integer literal compared with an expression of type text`, `n = lower(s)` is `an expression of type text compared with the column t.n of type INT`, `lower(s) = n + 1` is `an expression of type integer compared with an expression of type text`, `lower(s) = ? + 1` with NULL is `an expression of type null compared with an expression of type text`, `update t set n = lower(?)` with NULL is `an expression of type text for the column t.n of type INT`, and `update t set b = hex(b)` is `... for the column t.b of type BLOB`. Arithmetic whose every value is NULL has its own class now, `classNumber`, which meets numbers only, so arithmetic and text never meet whatever their arguments; `meetsArithmetic` and `arithmeticClass` are gone, and `comparable` holds the whole rule. The refusals inside text are the evaluator's, at their offsets in the whole text, columns before arguments within one side, each side in the order of the text: `column of type INT in a concatenation`, `column of type BLOB in a call of lower`, `column of type INT in a call of hex`, `argument 1 in a concatenation, which is an integer`, `argument 1 in a call of lower, which is a blob`, `argument 1 in a call of hex, which is a real`. NULL gives what the evaluator gives: `NULL || 'a'` and `lower(NULL)` are NULL, which compare with nothing and which a column that does not take NULL refuses with SQLite's `NOT NULL constraint failed`, and `hex(NULL)` is the empty text.
  - **A comparison of text** compares the bytes, as the collation BINARY does: `scalar` (the `number` of arithmetic, with a text) shares its bytes with the row or the value of the evaluator, so a condition still allocates nothing of its own.
  - **The order of the names, SQLite's.** `resolver` in `internal/engine/expression.go` walks the names as `resolveExprStep` does: a missing column inside the argument of a call does not end the walk but is pending, as one under IS NULL was already; a later missing column replaces it; a column that the table has and a call go on beside it; and any other node, an operator, a literal, a parameter, or the next comparison, ends the walk with it. A subquery that the walk reaches with a pending column finds its table, in an update and a delete, and looks no column up, and in a select, which found its table before, looks the column of its select list up; then the pending column ends the walk (`subqueries.pendingNames`). So `delete from t where hex(nosuch) = nosuch2` gives `nosuch2`, `delete from t where hex(nosuch) = s` gives `nosuch`, `update t set s = lower(nosuch1), nosuch2 = 1` gives `nosuch1`, `lower(nosuch) = s and nosuch2 is null` gives `nosuch2`, `lower(nosuch) = 'a' and nosuch2 is null` gives `nosuch`, and `delete from t where lower(nosuch1) <> (select nosuch3 from w)` gives `nosuch1` where the same select gives `nosuch3`. `TestEngine_text` compares 34 such statements with SQLite. No statement is refused for its names.
  - **The length limit.** `bindExpression` passes the database's limit (`build.limit`) to `Program.Bind`. `expr.ErrTooLong` is SQLite's own `string or blob too big` (`tooBig`, code 18), which `fromExpr` gives for it: an `*s4ql.Error` through the driver, an error of SQLite for the count of changes and the event, and not an evaluation error. No exported name was added.
  - **When the too-long error comes, as SQLite gives it.** SQLite computes a constant once: a constant without a call in the start of the statement, before any row (`sqlite3ExprCodeRunJustOnce`, which factors it out), and a constant with a call behind an `OP_Once`, when the first row reaches it, unless it serves the access, which computes it before the search. The engine gives the error in the same places:
    - A concatenation without a column and without a call inside an expression of text with a column, or inside a value of SET, is computed once before any row (`hoisted`, `build.hoist`, `computeHoisted`): `update t set u = ? || ? || s`, `update t set u = s || (? || ?)`, and `delete from t where ? || ? || s = 'x'` fail on an empty table, `update t set u = s || ? || ?` and `where lower(? || ?) || s = 'x'` do not.
    - A value of SET is computed for each row that the update changes, as SQLite does: a constant of text that is too long is computed again for each row and fails on the first (`update t set u = ? || ?` and `= lower(? || ?)` give no error on an empty table, or with `where k = 99`).
    - A side of a comparison without a column is computed before the access, as for arithmetic, where it has no call (`where u = ? || ?` fails on an empty table and with `limit 0`), and where it has a call and may serve the access: a column alone with an operator other than `!=`, of the key or of a column of an index (`mayServe`). Otherwise a constant with a call that is too long keeps its error (`condition.failed`), serves no access, and gives it on the first row that its condition is evaluated on: `where u = 'zz' and v = lower(? || ?)` gives no error where no row has `u = 'zz'`, `where v = lower(? || ?) and u = 'zz'` fails on the first row, and `where lower(u) = lower(? || ?)` and `where u <> lower(? || ?)` give no error on an empty table.
    - In a subquery, a side without a column and without a call is computed before any row of the statement, also where the statement does not compute the subquery (`k is null`, LIMIT 0, a subquery of LIMIT 0), unless it may serve the access of the subquery, where it is computed with the subquery.
    - An expression with a column is computed for each row, and fails on the row whose text is too long, also `hex` of a text of 500 bytes under a limit of 1,000.
    - In a read-only transaction, an update or a delete computes nothing and gives `attempt to write a readonly database`, as for arithmetic.
  - **The access.** A text expression without a column serves the lookup of the key and the bounds of an index as an argument does, and one with a column serves nothing: on a table `q (id text primary key, v text)` with an index on `v`, `id = 'm_' || ?`, `id = 'm_' || lower(hex(?))`, and `id = lower(?)` read one row by the key, `id > 'm_' || ? and id < 'n'` reads the range, `v = lower(?)` reads by the index, `id = ? || ?` with NULL reads nothing, and `lower(id) = ?`, `id = 'm_' || v`, and `v = lower(w)` read every row.
  - **The shared rules.** `stmttest.TextRefusal` is gone. `stmttest.ArithmeticRefusals` types text as the engine does (`expressionClass` and `textRefusals`), with the class text and the refusals of the places of the evaluator, and requires numbers only beside arithmetic (`isArithmetic`), where it required them beside any expression; `ArithmeticRefusal` knows the refusals inside text and no longer the whole refusals of text. `stmttest.Quirk` gives a concatenation and a call no quirk. `stmttest.MissingColumn` walks the names of the sides of a comparison as the engine's `resolver` does. `TestArithmeticRefusals` has 22 cases of text, where it had 5 that expected the whole refusal. The rule of the driver check for refusals, which failed on `where lower(v1) = 'a'` prepared and then run, holds now that `Prepare` takes text; the hand sequence has it.
  - **The equivalent mutants.** `internal/sql/mutation/equivalent.txt` lost five entries of `columnClass` and `valueClass`, whose reason, that text and blobs were refused alike, no longer holds, and the entry of `meetsArithmetic`; the entry of `build.arithmetic` names its new line.
- **Where s4ql is stricter than SQLite or differs from it, in the engine and the driver,** for the lead to tell Markus:
  - Text compared with a number, set into a column of a number or a blob, or meeting arithmetic is refused from the types, where SQLite orders or converts.
  - A constant of text with a call that may serve the access fails before any row, where SQLite computes it only when it uses it: on a table whose `s` has an index, `select k from t where k = 99 and s = lower(? || ?)` fails with `string or blob too big`, and SQLite, which finds the row by the key, gives no row. `TestEngine_text` pins it.
  - A scalar subquery is computed whenever the statement may give a row, as for arithmetic, where SQLite computes it when a row reaches it: `select k from t where k = 99 and u = (select s from t where s = ? || ?)`, with an index on `s`, fails in s4ql and gives no row in SQLite. `TestEngine_text` pins it.
  - **A statement that fails with `string or blob too big` in a transaction changes nothing in s4ql, and the transaction goes on, as after every other error. SQLite keeps the rows that the statement changed before the row that failed, where it holds no statement journal for it,** which it opens only for a statement that may abort on a constraint: in a write transaction, `update t set s = s || s` of the rows `'a'` and 600 bytes of `'b'` under a limit of 1,000 fails in both, and SQLite's row of `'a'` is `'aa'` after it, where s4ql's is `'a'`. Measured with the shell, an update of a column that takes NULL, also with an index on it and in a rowid table, keeps the change, and one of a column of NOT NULL takes it back. The reads after it in the transaction then differ. This is new with text, whose error SQLite gives in the middle of a statement; arithmetic gave none of SQLite's. `TestEngine_text` pins it. Copying it would copy SQLite's choice of a statement journal and of a one-pass update, so the step keeps the rule of arithmetic; **it is a question of design for the lead and Markus.**
  - The rows that a statement evaluates are those of its access, so where the engine's access reads a row that SQLite's does not, or the other way round, one fails with `string or blob too big` and the other does not; SQLite also evaluates the terms of a WHERE that an index covers before the others, and those with a subquery last, where the engine evaluates them in the order of the text. No statement of the tests shows a difference there; the generated checks are the place to look.
  - Nowhere does s4ql give another value than SQLite for a row that both evaluate.
