# Performance measurement

- **Agreed on 2026-11-20, not built: performance measurement.** It has two kinds.
  - Counts, which are exact: the rows read from the cache per query, the requests and bytes to S3 per commit, and allocations. They are pinned in tests and fail on a regression, and they never depend on timing. The first are built with slice 9 of the [roadmap](../roadmap.md): `TestEngine_rowsRead` pins the rows read from the cache for a point lookup, an equality, an equality with a range, and goqite's receive without its `order by`, with and without an index, and the README of the engine has the table. The requests and bytes to S3 and the allocations are not built.
  - Timings, which are noisy: Go benchmarks of statements, commits, and replay. Each runs beside the same work on plain SQLite on the same machine, and is reported as the ratio to SQLite. CI records them on every merge to `main`, and a chart shows them over time. This is a piece of work of its own, after slice 9.
  - Not decided: where the results are kept, and where the chart lives.

- **Found with slice 13 on 2026-11-21, not built: three ways to make a query with `order by` cheaper.** None changes a result or a format. The decisions of the slice are in [its todo](slice-13.md).
  - A sort carries whole rows, and every row within the bounds of the access, also those that the filter then drops, because the cache's SQLite sorts and the filter is the engine's. SQLite's own plan for goqite's receive without goqite's index sorts three columns and keeps one row. A sort of the key and the ordered columns alone, with the rows fetched after it, or a limit pushed into the cache's statement where the conditions are exactly the pins and the bound of the access, would read the same rows and hold less.
  - The engine has the cache sort every row or none. Where the key of the access delivers the first columns of the `order by` and not the rest, SQLite could sort within the groups of the first columns and stop early under a `limit`. The rows read would then depend on the data in a way that the engine cannot say without counting in the cache.
  - An `order by` is not cut after the primary key, and with goqite's index and no message ready, goqite's receive reads every message of its queue, where an index on `(queue, timeout)` would read none. The rules have no statistics, so they cannot tell the two cases apart.
