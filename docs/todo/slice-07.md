# Slice 7: `where` on `select`

The decisions and notes of the builders of [slice 7 of the roadmap](../roadmap.md#slice-7-where-on-select).

## The statement pass

- **Decided by the builder of the pass for slice 7 on 2026-11-20, built, for the lead to confirm:**
  - `stmt.Select` has `Where []stmt.Condition`: the conditions in the order of the text, all joined by AND, and nil without WHERE. A `stmt.Condition` has `Column`, a `Name`, `Operator`, and `Value`, a `stmt.Term` as for a value of an insert. For `OpIsNull` and `OpIsNotNull` the `Value` is the zero `Term`. `stmt.Operator` has eight constants, and its `String` gives `=`, `!=`, `<`, `<=`, `>`, `>=`, `IS NULL`, and `IS NOT NULL`. `=` and `==` are one operator, and `!=` and `<>` are one: SQLite's tokenizer gives each pair one kind of token, `TK_EQ` and `TK_NE`, and nothing that s4ql shows prints the operator, since the annotation keeps the text of the statement itself. `Parameters()` of a select counts the `?` of its conditions, and the k-th `?` of the text has the number k.
  - The new names of what is not supported: `value on the left of a comparison`, `column compared with a column`, `column with the name of a table`, `OR`, `IS DISTINCT FROM`, `IS with a value other than NULL`, and `WHERE deeper than SQLite's limit`. `NOT operator` and `TRUE or FALSE` cover WHERE too. Each of these is at the exact token: the value after IS, the DISTINCT, the column on the right, the first OR, after the conditions before it, and the AND that makes the WHERE too deep. A form that `describe` names by its node, such as `parentheses`, `binary operator`, `function call`, `IN`, `BETWEEN`, and `COLLATE`, is at the start of its construct, as for INSERT.
  - `isnull`, `notnull`, and `not null` after a column stay `NULL test`, and IS with anything but NULL is not supported, as the lead asked: the roadmap lists only `is null` and `is not null`.
  - A WHERE deeper than SQLite's limit is not supported. SQLite refuses an expression deeper than 1,000, `SQLITE_MAX_EXPR_DEPTH`, while it parses, with `Expression tree is too large (maximum depth 1000)`, whatever the database holds. It is an error of a grammar action, and the pass rejects such errors as before. A condition is 2 deep, and 3 with a sign before its value, and an AND is one deeper than its deeper side. So 999 conditions are the most, and 998 with a sign in one of the first two.
  - Facts of SQLite for the builder of the engine, each checked against SQLite by `checkWhere` in the tests of the pass. A comparison with NULL, as a literal or as an argument, gives no rows. SQLite looks for the names of the select list first, then for those of WHERE in the order of the text, and the first comparison of a column that the table does not have gives `no such column`. A missing column under IS NULL or IS NOT NULL does not stop it: `resolveExprStep` walks the operand of `TK_ISNULL` itself and ignores its abort. A later test for NULL of a missing column then replaces the message, and the next comparison stops the walk before SQLite looks for its own column. `a is null and b is null` gives `no such column: b`, and `a is null and b = 1` gives `no such column: a`. The rows come in key order: SQLite searches the primary key for a condition on it, and scans in key order otherwise. `rowid`, `oid`, and `_rowid_` give `no such column` in a WITHOUT ROWID table, unless a column has that name.
  - `Generator.Conditions` of `stmttest` turns on the forms of slice 7: with n, one select in two has a WHERE of 1 to n conditions. `Generator.Where` is the hook that gives the conditions of a select of a table. With `Conditions` at 0 and `Where` nil the generator draws as before: a dump of 63,000 texts and arguments of `Generator` and `Sequence` from 30 seeds is the same on the old code and the new, byte for byte. The pass's tests set `Conditions` to 4. `stmttest.Sequence` set neither then, so the tests of the engine and the driver drew the same calls. It sets the hook `Where` since the generated checks of slice 7, of the same day.
  - The pinned numbers of the pass's generated tests moved, only because the generator draws other random numbers with `Conditions` on. The README of the pass has them, with the reasons, under "Results". The pinned numbers of the tests of the engine and of `drivercheck` did not move.
  - Until the engine ran slice 7, it refused a select with WHERE as not supported, with `checkWhere` in `internal/engine/errors.go`, as `checkNulls` did for slice 6, and `NewPlan` panicked for such a select. `checkWhere` and the `checkText` wrappers of the tests are gone since the engine and the driver run the slice; see the next item. The driver's `check` in `conn.go` calls `stmt.Check` alone again, without the second parse through `engine.Check`, which refuses none of the forms of slice 7. `Prepare` still refuses at once what the text alone shows is not supported, such as `or` and `order by`.

## The engine and the driver

- **Decided by the builder of the engine and the driver for slice 7 on 2026-11-20, built, for the lead to confirm.** The README of the engine has the details under "The stages", "The storage interface", "The operators", "Errors", "Parameters", "Types", and "How it is tested". The order of the errors was confirmed by running SQLite, and `TestEngine_where` pins it.
  - The plan. A select with WHERE has a `*Filter` below its `*Project`, with `Source` and `Conditions`. A `Condition` has `Column`, a `stmt.Name`, `Operator`, a `stmt.Operator`, and `Value`, an expression, which is nil for IS NULL and IS NOT NULL. `Format` prints the filter on a line of its own under `project`, such as `filter v1 >= ?1 and v2 IS NOT NULL`, with `scan t1` below it. `NewPlan` no longer panics for WHERE.
  - The comparisons. The operator `filter` gives the rows of its source for which every condition is true, in the order of the source. A comparison with NULL on either side, which is a NULL in the row, the literal NULL, or a nil argument, is unknown, and the WHERE leaves the row out. IS NULL and IS NOT NULL are always true or false. `compare` compares two values of one type as SQLite does: integers and reals by their number, with 0.0 equal to -0.0, and text and blobs by their bytes, as memcmp does, with the shorter first, which is SQLite's BINARY collation. Values of two kinds are an internal error, which the exact types rule out.
  - The use of the key. `keyAccess` gives the `scan` its `access`: no row at all if a comparison is with NULL or the key is tested with IS NULL, since the key of a WITHOUT ROWID table is never NULL; a `get` of the row of the key for the first `key = value`; otherwise a `scanRange` from the tightest lower bound to the tightest upper bound of the conditions on the key with `<`, `<=`, `>`, and `>=`, where of two bounds at the same value the open one is tighter; and otherwise a scan of every row. `!=`, a condition on another column, and IS NOT NULL read every row. The filter tests every condition on every row that the access gives, so the access can leave out only rows that some condition rejects: the rows and their order are those of a scan of every row.
  - The core has `core.Bound`, `ReadTx.ScanRange`, and `Tx.ScanRange`: a scan from a lower to an upper bound, each closed, open, or none, of a table whose key is one column. `Scan` is `ScanRange` without bounds. A bound is checked as the key of `Get` is, with `ErrInvalid` and `ErrTooBig`. The cache runs the bounds as `where k >= ?1 and k < ?2` on its query in key order. The engine's storage interface `reader` has `scanRange(ctx, name, lower, upper bound)`, and `fake` and `memory` have it. `TestScanRange` in `internal/core/cache_test.go` compares every pair of bounds, at every edge value of each type, with a filter of the full scan, in a read transaction and in a write transaction.
  - `engine.Options` has the field `scanAll`, which tests set through `Options.ScanAll` in `export_test.go`, so that every query reads every row and tests its conditions on each. `TestEngine_where` runs every query on an engine that reads by the key, on one with `scanAll`, and on SQLite. `TestDifferential` and `FuzzDifferential_core` take the flag `-scanall`. The driver's public options have no such knob, so `drivercheck` runs by the key only; the driver goes through the same engine.
  - The order of the errors of a select with WHERE: the errors of the text and of the arguments, as before, which come before the instance; `no such table`; `no such column` for the first name of the select list that the table does not have; the names of the conditions in the order of the text; `string or blob too big` for a value of a condition that is longer than the length limit, an argument, for which SQLite gives it when it binds, or a literal; and last "not supported" for the first value of a condition, in the order of the text, that is not NULL and is of another type than its column, also where SQLite would convert it, such as `'1'` for an INT column and `1` for a REAL column.
  - The names of the conditions. The first comparison of a column that the table does not have gives `no such column: x`. A missing column under IS NULL or IS NOT NULL does not stop SQLite: a later such test replaces the message, and the next comparison ends the walk with it, before SQLite looks its own column up. `x is null and y is null` gives `y`, `x is null and y = 1` gives `x`, and `x is not null and a is null and y is not null and a = 2` gives `y`, where the table has `a`. A name in double quotes gets `no such column: "x" - should this be a string literal in single-quotes?`. `rowid`, `oid`, and `_rowid_` give `no such column`.
  - The messages of the refusal: `s4ql: not supported: a string literal compared with the column t.a of type INT` (or `an integer literal`, `a real literal`, `a blob literal`), and `s4ql: not supported: argument 1, which is text, compared with the column t.a of type INT`, with the names as the table stores them. A NULL value has no type and is never refused. The refusal comes in a read-only transaction too, because a select reads there. A select leaves the count of changes, also when it fails.
  - A literal of a condition that is longer than the length limit gets `string or blob too big`, as in an insert. SQLite gives the same for a string literal and no rows for a blob literal; see the known difference in [sql-front-end.md](sql-front-end.md), beside the item on `statement too long`.
  - Parameters are numbered across the conditions in the order of the text. The annotation is not involved, since a select writes nothing.
  - The rules of the generated tests, in `internal/sql/stmt/stmttest/rule.go`. `ConditionRule` gives s4ql's refusal for the first condition whose value is of another type than its column, and "" where SQLite's error of a name comes first or the number of arguments differs. `MissingColumn` gives the name of SQLite's `no such column` for a select, by SQLite's walk. The engine's `typeRule` and the simulation take a select with WHERE through `ConditionRule`, and `pair.checkType` applies the rule to a select in a read-only transaction too. `typeRefusal` of `drivercheck` uses it as well. Its `checkQuoted` now covers a name in double quotes in a condition, by `MissingColumn`: mattn's SQLite reads such a name as a string there too.
  - Four pinned numbers of the generated tests moved, with the generator drawing as before. `stmttest.Sequence` sets no conditions, so the only texts with WHERE are the two of `stmttest.Others`, `select * from %[1]v where %[2]v = ?` and `select * from %[1]v where %[2]v = %[3]v`, which the engine refused as not supported and now runs. In the engine's `TestDifferential`, `excusedType` went from 339 to 350: 11 such selects compare a value of another type than the column. In `TestGenerated` of `drivercheck`, `excusedPrepare` went from 1060 to 1063, for 3 that compare a column that the table does not have, which SQLite gives at prepare and s4ql when the statement runs; `excusedType` from 161 to 169, for 8 that compare a value of another type; and `excusedQuoted` from 45 to 48, for 3 that compare a name in double quotes that is no column. No other pinned number moved.

## The generated checks

- **Decided by the builder of the generated checks for slice 7 on 2026-11-20, built, for the lead to confirm.** The README of the engine has the details under "How it is tested" and "Results". The checks found no difference from SQLite or from mattn, and none between the two ways of reading rows.
  - The forms and the rates of the WHERE. `stmttest.Sequence` sets the hook `Generator.Where`, which is `Sequence.where`, and leaves `Generator.Conditions` at 0. One select in two has a WHERE. One WHERE in five is a range of the key: a lower and an upper bound, each open or closed, in either order, with none to two more conditions. If the value of the lower bound is above that of the upper one, the two change places three times in four, and the fourth time the range holds no key. The other WHERE have one or two conditions, or four to seven one time in eight. The column of a condition is that of an earlier condition one time in four, a name that the shape does not have one time in ten, which is in double quotes in some spellings, the key one time in three, and any column of the shape otherwise. The operator is IS NULL one time in twelve, IS NOT NULL one time in twelve, and otherwise one of the six comparisons. One WHERE in eight gets one more condition, the inverse of one of its own, which contradicts it.
  - The values of the conditions, from `Sequence.conditionValue`, so that a condition is often true of some rows and false of others. A value is NULL one time in twenty, and of another type than its column one time in twenty. Two times in five it is a value that an insert of the sequence gave the column, and one time in eight a neighbour of such a value. One time in ten it is an edge of the type, and otherwise a value as an insert gets it. `Sequence.store` keeps the last twelve values that the inserts gave each column, whether SQLite took the insert or not, and draws no random number for it. `neighbour` gives the next integer or real above or below, and for text and a blob a prefix, the least value above, or the value with its last character or byte one more or one less. `edgeOf` gives the limits of 64 bits, 0, -1, and 1 for an integer; 0.0, -0.0, and the largest and the smallest finite reals of each sign for a real; the empty text and the least and the greatest code point for text; and the empty blob, one zero byte, and one byte of 255 for a blob. The generator writes one value in three as a `?`, so NULL comes as a literal and as a nil argument, and `Sequence.Next` puts the rejected arguments around such calls as before.
  - `stmttest.WhereCases` and `stmttest.KeyAccess`, in `rule.go`. `WhereCases` names what a select with WHERE reaches: each operator on the key or on another column, each kind of value as a literal or as an argument, a column that the table does not have, also in double quotes, a value of another type, a column tested more than once, contradicting conditions, and how the plan reads the rows. `KeyAccess` is the roadmap's rule of the use of the key, written a second time, apart from the engine's `keyAccess`: no row, a get of one key, a scan from a lower bound, to an upper bound, or between two bounds, also two bounds that hold no key, or a scan of every row. The engine's `TestDifferential` and `drivercheck` share `WhereCases`.
  - Both ways of reading rows, in `pair.step`. `pair.bothWays` ran every call that holds a select with WHERE a second time on the same engine, with `Engine.SetScanAll(true)` of `export_test.go`, and required the same answer, message, and count of changes; since the generated checks of slice 8 it runs every call on a second engine that reads the other way, as the decisions of that builder in [slice-08.md](slice-08.md) say. So every test that goes through `pair.step` reads by the key and reads every row, without a flag: `TestDifferential`, `TestConn_differential`, in transactions too, `FuzzDifferential`, `FuzzDifferential_core`, `FuzzSQL`, `FuzzTypes`, and `FuzzWhere`, with their `_core` targets. A query reads the setting when it starts. The flag `-scanall` still makes every query of `TestDifferential` and `FuzzDifferential_core` read every row, and the second engine then reads by the key.
  - The cross-check of the access. A comparison of rows cannot tell a plan that reads every row from one that reads by the key. So `reachWhere` compares `stmttest.KeyAccess` with `engine.AccessOf` of `export_test.go`, which gives the access that `buildQuery` gives the scan, for each select with WHERE that the rule decides, and a plan that reads more rows than the rule says fails. The check runs in the tests that count what they reach, `TestDifferential` and `TestConn_differential`, and not in the fuzz targets, which have no tally. A temporary mutant that dropped the `get` of `keyAccess` failed `TestDifferential` at once by it.
  - A literal in a condition that is longer than the length limit. `pair.checkType` leaves such a select to the comparison with SQLite (`pair.longCondition`), because s4ql refuses it before the type of a value, and SQLite mostly too. `pair.step` excuses a difference, as `excusedLongCondition`, only where SQLite gives no error and s4ql gives `string or blob too big`, which is the known difference in [sql-front-end.md](sql-front-end.md); where SQLite gives no error, s4ql must give `string or blob too big`, so a long blob literal, which gives no rows on both sides if s4ql drops the check, is checked against the rule. The default run of `TestDifferential` reaches 2 such selects, SQLite refuses both too, and the excuse is pinned at 0. `reachedDifferential` requires one such select.
  - `TestGenerated` of `drivercheck` counts the same cases, in `pair.reachWhere`, and requires a least number of each, a third of what the run gave. It counts a call with the wrong number of arguments and one with an argument that s4ql does not support together, as "WHERE with arguments that s4ql refuses". It reads by the key only, and has no cross-check of the access: the driver has no public knob that makes a query read every row, and `AccessOf` is of the engine's tests.
  - The simulation. Every second engine of a node reads every row (`run.open`, by the generation of the open): the first engine of a node reads by the key, the second, which opens after a crash or a close, reads every row, and so on in turn. So the checks against SQLite see both ways of reading rows, also across crashes and closes. The hammer workload opens its one instance once, so it reads by the key only. `run.countWhere` counts the selects with WHERE that `run.verify` and `run.verifyTxs` compare with SQLite, of their own and in a transaction, those of them on an engine that reads every row, and those of them that give rows. `TestSimulation` requires a least number of each for each seed, a quarter of what the first 1,000 seeds gave. The selects of their own were 3,078 in the default workload, 1,107 of them on an engine that reads every row and 324 that give rows; 2,449, 786, and 58 in the idle workload; and 6,968, 857 of which give rows, in the hammer workload. The selects in a transaction were 4,254, 1,507, and 468; 2,875, 892, and 78; and 8,052 and 1,041. For the 58 and the 78, the least number is one in fifty seeds.
  - The planted bug `openBound` makes a scan by one or two bounds of the key read a closed bound, of `<=` or `>=`, as open, so that it leaves out the row whose key is the bound. `bugs.reader` in `bugs.go` wraps the reader of a query in `openBounds`, in `Conn.query` and in `Conn.queryIn`, so a statement of its own and one in a transaction both plant it, and the signature of `buildQuery` did not change. Its own violation is ", and in SQLite": a select gives fewer rows than SQLite. Nothing else sees it, because a select writes nothing and fails nothing. An engine that reads every row calls no `scanRange`, and does not have the bug. 20 of 200 seeds of the default workload found it, each by its own violation. The engine has thirteen planted bugs now. With updates and deletes in the sequence, which take turns of the selects and delete rows, the default workload found it in 8 of 200 seeds, so since the mutation testing of slice 8 it runs in the hammer workload, where 32 of 200 find it.
  - `FuzzWhere` and `FuzzWhere_core`. The first byte of the input chooses a table `w_<type>` of one of the five types, with a key `k` and a column `v` that takes NULL. Its rows have keys at the edges of the type, and one row in three has `v` NULL. `whereOf` builds a WHERE of up to eight conditions from the other bytes: on `k`, on `v`, or on `x`, which the table does not have, also in double quotes; with every spelling of the operators; and with the key of a row, a value of the type from the bytes, NULL, or a value of another type, as a literal or as an argument. `pair.step` compares the select with SQLite and reads it both ways. A run of one minute of each found nothing, in 222,955 and 11,619 executions.
  - The pinned numbers moved. In the engine's `TestDifferential`: the texts of a table that exists from 18 to 19, the calls with the wrong number of arguments from 703 to 718, those with an argument that no column holds from 220 to 248, the values of another type from 350 to 478, and the literals that are too long from 8 to 4. The sequence draws other random numbers, which moves each. The fourth also went up because 112 selects of the sequence compare a value of another type than the column, and the second and the third because a WHERE has parameters, whose arguments `Sequence.Next` makes wrong now and then. In `TestGenerated` of `drivercheck`: `excusedPrepare` from 1063 to 1178, `excusedStartedTable` from 10 to 11, `excusedArguments` from 426 to 411, `excusedArgument` from 200 to 260, `excusedType` from 169 to 231, `excusedLevel` from 74 to 69, `excusedRowsOpen` from 46 to 24, `excusedTxOpen` from 29 to 24, `excusedOutside` from 53 to 58, and `excusedQuoted` from 48 to 69. A WHERE names a column that the table does not have now and then, which SQLite gives at prepare and s4ql when the statement runs, and which took `excusedPrepare` up, and `excusedQuoted` for those in double quotes. A select with WHERE fails more often than one without, so fewer rows are left open in a transaction, which took `excusedRowsOpen` down. Both tests require a least number of each case of slice 7, a third of what the run gave, and `TestConn_differential` requires 146 selects that are read both ways, 32 of them in a read-only transaction, a third of the 438 and the 96 of its run. The counts of the planted bugs over 200 seeds moved too; the README of the engine has them under "Results".

## Not built yet

- **What is left of slice 7 in the engine and the driver.** `drivercheck` cannot read both ways, because the driver's public options have no knob that makes a query read every row: `TestGenerated` reads by the key only. `core.Bound` holds the value of a key of one column; a key of several columns would need a bound of several values.
- **Not built, for a fresh builder: long fuzz runs of the code of slice 7 in the pass** (`FuzzCheck`, `FuzzGenerate`, and `FuzzLiteral`). Its mutation testing is done, with that of the engine, the core, and the driver; the results are in [the README of the runner](../../internal/sql/mutation/README.md#the-where-of-slice-7).
