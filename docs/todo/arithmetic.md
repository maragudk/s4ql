# Arithmetic

The decisions and notes of the builders of the piece of work "arithmetic", the first step into [expressions](expressions.md): s4ql's own evaluator, and `+`, `-`, `*`, and `/` on integers and reals in SET and in WHERE, as the [roadmap](../roadmap.md#expressions-and-functions) has it. Goqite's receive, `update goqite set timeout = ?, received = received + 1 where ...`, is the milestone, not the specification.

## The statement pass

- **Decisions of the statement pass of arithmetic, by its builder on 2026-11-25, built, for the lead to confirm.** Each fact of SQLite here was measured with the pinned shell (3.54.1), and the tests of the pass check it against SQLite: `checkExpressions` in `internal/sql/stmt/stmt_test.go` on every supported text with an expression, and the subtests of `TestCheck` named below. [The README of the pass](../../internal/sql/stmt/README.md) has the forms token for token.
  - **An expression** is built from a column (bare or in double quotes), an integer or real literal, a plain `?`, parentheses, the binary `+`, `-`, `*`, and `/`, and a `-` before an operand. The tree is SQLite's: its parser builds it, with `*` and `/` before `+` and `-`, each grouping to the left, and the `-` of a negation binding most tightly.
  - **Where.** As the value of an assignment of SET, and as either side of a comparison of a WHERE, of a select, an update, a delete, and of a scalar subquery, with each of the six operators. Nowhere else: the select list, VALUES, ORDER BY, LIMIT, RETURNING, DEFAULT, and the operand of IS keep their refusals (`result column other than a column or *`, `binary operator`, `ordering term other than a column`, `LIMIT of an expression`, `DEFAULT of an expression`, and `binary operator` or `unary operator` before IS).
  - **The typed form, the contract for the engine.** `stmt.Expression` is one node: `Kind` (`ExprValue`, `ExprParameter`, `ExprColumn`, `ExprNegate`, `ExprAdd`, `ExprSubtract`, `ExprMultiply`, `ExprDivide`), with `Value`, `Parameter`, or `Column` for a leaf, `Operands []stmt.Expression` for an operator, and `Span`. It holds its operands by value in a slice, so no statement holds a pointer and `%#v` prints the whole tree. A later operator, a function, CASE, or AND, OR, and NOT is a new kind with its operands in the same slice, so the type need not change. Parentheses are no nodes. A `-` directly before a number is part of the literal, as SQLite codes it: `-5` is `ExprValue` -5, and `-9223372036854775808` the smallest integer. `stmt.Term.Expression` holds an expression as the value of an assignment or of a comparison; its `Value` is then nil, `Parameter` 0, and `Subquery` 0, and its `Span` holds the parentheses around the expression. `stmt.Condition.Expression` holds the side that a condition tests where that side is an expression; its `Column` is then the zero Name.
  - **How the engine tells the cases apart.** A plain value is a `Term` whose `Expression.Present()` is false: a literal, a `?`, or a subquery, exactly as before, so the planning of key lookups and index bounds reads it as before. An expression without a column is `Term.Expression.Present() && !Term.Expression.HasColumn()`: it has one value for each run of the statement, from its literals and arguments, so it can serve a lookup or a bound as a `?` does, once the engine has computed it. An expression with a column is `Term.Expression.HasColumn()`, or any `Condition.Expression.Present()`: it has a value for each row, so a condition with one is a filter, and no lookup. A plain column is `!Condition.Expression.Present()`, with `Condition.Column`, as before.
  - **Which side a condition tests.** The side with a column. Where both sides have one, it is the side that is a column alone, or the left side where neither is one. So `a + 1 = b` is the condition on `b` with `ValueFirst` and the value `a + 1`, and `a * 2 < b * 3` tests `a * 2` with the value `b * 3`. `ValueFirst` and the swapped operator follow the rule of slice 12. `Condition.Expression` is present only where neither side is a column alone.
  - **Comparisons that stay refused.** A column alone on both sides is `column compared with a column`, as before, also where one of them is in a comparison that an expression would allow. No column on either side is `value compared with a value`, as before, and an expression without a column is a value there: `1 + 1 = 2` and `? * 2 = 1 + ?`. The name comes at the right side; for an expression without a column, once the pass has read it.
  - **Parentheses** are supported around an operator, at most 100 open at once. Around a single literal, parameter, column, or subquery, or around parentheses, they are `parentheses`, as before: SQLite reads them as what they hold, and a column in parentheses would make `(a) = b` a column compared with a column. The limit of 100 is s4ql's own, `parentheses deeper than 100`, at the 101st: SQLite's parser holds each open parenthesis and each operator that waits for its right operand on a stack of 2,499 entries, and gives `Recursion limit` beyond it, from 831 levels of `(1 + (1 + ...))`, while the parser of s4ql reads 2,499. With at most 100 open, no second sign, and two levels of binary operators, an expression holds far fewer entries.
  - **Refused from the text, each at the literal or the token named:** a string, a blob, or NULL as an operand of `+`, `-`, `*`, or `/`, as `string in arithmetic`, `blob in arithmetic`, and `NULL in arithmetic` (after a `-`, they keep `string after a sign`, `blob after a sign`, and `NULL after a sign`); a division by the literal 0 or 0.0, with or without a sign, or by a real that SQLite reads as 0.0 such as `1e-400`, as `division by zero`, at the literal (SQLite gives NULL); an integer literal outside 64 bits, as `integer outside 64 bits`, also after a binary `-`: `a - 9223372036854775808` is a real in SQLite, while `a - -9223372036854775808` and `-9223372036854775808 * a` are the smallest integer; a second sign, `- -v`, as `second sign`; a `+`, `~`, or NOT before an operand, as `+ sign`, `~ operator`, and `NOT operator`; and every other operator (`%`, `||`, `&`, a comparison inside an operand) as `binary operator`, a function as `function call`, CAST, CASE, COLLATE, and a subquery as an operand as `subquery`.
  - **The depth.** A literal, a parameter, and a column are 1 deep, a literal after a `-` 2 deep, every operator one more than its deeper operand, and parentheses nothing, as SQLite counts. In WHERE, a comparison is one more than its deeper side, and the limit of 1,000 holds for the whole WHERE, as `WHERE deeper than SQLite's limit`; in SET each value has a limit of its own, as `expression deeper than SQLite's limit`. `a = 1 + 1 + ...` takes 998 `+` and SET takes 999, one fewer each where the first operand is a negative literal. In a subquery, SQLite adds the WHERE of the statement and that of the subquery once it has found the tables, as for slice 15: `k = (select k from u where a = 1 + ...)` takes 497 `+`. The subtests "should take an expression as deep as SQLite allows, and not one deeper" and "should take 100 parentheses open at once, and not 101, of which SQLite's parser takes either" of `TestCheck` check each edge against SQLite.
  - **Comments.** `a--5` is the column `a` and a comment, as in SQLite; `a - -5` and `a -\n-5` subtract -5.
  - **Names that changed.** In SET and in a comparison, `-?` (was `parameter after a sign`), `-v` and `5 < -v` (were `unary operator`), `v = w` in SET and `v = "a"` there (were `column reference`), and the arithmetic of `binary operator` are supported now. `~1 < v` is `~ operator` (was `unary operator`), `- -5 = v` is `second sign` (was `unary operator`), and `update t set v = u.v from u ...` is `column with the name of a table` (was `column reference`).
- **Where s4ql is stricter than SQLite or differs from it, in the statement pass,** for the lead to tell Markus:
  - A string, a blob, or NULL literal as an operand of arithmetic is refused. SQLite reads `'1' + 1` as 2 and `'abc' + 1` as 1, and gives NULL for `a + null`. The refusal of text is agreed; that of the literal NULL is the lead's brief, since such an expression is always NULL.
  - A division by a literal zero is refused, where SQLite gives NULL. Agreed as a rule; the pass applies it to the text.
  - An integer literal outside 64 bits is refused, also where SQLite reads it as a real, as before.
  - A second sign, `- -v` or `- -5`, is refused; SQLite negates twice.
  - Parentheses around a single value or around parentheses are refused; SQLite reads them as what they hold.
  - More than 100 parentheses open at once are refused; SQLite takes up to about 830 levels.
  - The error of a text that SQLite refuses is often another one: `Expression tree is too large` is `WHERE deeper than SQLite's limit` or `expression deeper than SQLite's limit`, and `Recursion limit` comes after the pass has refused the text for its parentheses.
- **Until the engine evaluates expressions,** `engine.expressionRefusal`, in `internal/engine/expression.go` and called in `engine.check`, refuses an update, a select, or a delete with an expression as `s4ql: not supported: expression`, at the start of its first expression in the order of the text. `engine.NewPlan` panics for such a statement. The driver's `check` calls `engine.Check` after `stmt.Check` again, as for slice 15, so `Prepare` refuses the text. A text that the pass refuses for a token of an expression gets the pass's error, such as `division by zero`. `TestCheck_expression` and `TestConn_expression` in `internal/engine/expression_test.go`, and `TestPrepare` in `conn_test.go`, pin it. The simulator's SQL box already calls `engine.Check` after `stmt.Check`. In the differential tests of the engine, `expressed` in `expression_test.go` lets `pair.step` take the refusal for a text that the pass supports, and skips the rules of values and `pair.everyPath` for it, as `subqueried` did for slice 15; `TestPair_expression` pins it. `FuzzSQL` can reach such a text from its seeds.
- **The generator.** `stmttest.Generator.Expression`, 0 by default: with it, one value of SET in so many is an expression, or one time in four a column alone; one comparison in so many tests an expression of its column, `-c` or `c op x` or `x op c` with an `x` without a column; and the value of one comparison in so many is an expression, with a column where the comparison tests a column alone, on either side. The operators are three levels deep at most, with parentheses where SQLite needs them and one time in four where it does not, and never around a single value; a literal on the right of `/` is never zero, and a column after a parenthesis is never `select`, `with`, or `values`. With 0 it draws as before: a dump of the texts and the arguments of `Generator.Statement` and of `Generator.CallOf` of every kind, in two settings over 30 seeds, 15,608 lines, was the same byte for byte before the change and after it. The pass's tests set it to 4, but for the near misses, whose forms use the text of the value of an assignment. `TestGenerator_Expression` checks that every statement is the pass's, and that every kind of node and every place comes.
- **The numbers that the pass's tests pin** moved only by the generator's draws: `TestGenerate_mutants` from 35 to 34 syntax errors and from 139 to 165 not supported where SQLite does not say. A tally by SQLite's message with `Expression` 0 and 4 found create tables only: with 4, 34 syntax errors of `duplicate column name`, and 143 of `unknown table option`, 21 of `duplicate column name`, and 1 of `no such collation sequence` not supported. The near misses keep 2,275 not supported where SQLite does not say: 11 forms that the pass now supports were replaced by forms that it refuses, and 7 forms of arithmetic that it refuses were added.
- **Facts of SQLite for the builder of the evaluator, measured with the pinned shell on 2026-11-25,** and what the pass leaves to the engine, since it needs the types of the columns or the arguments:
  - **The result.** Integer with integer is an integer, with a real a real. `7 / 2` is 3 and `-7 / 2` is -3: integer division truncates toward zero. An integer overflow gives a real: `9223372036854775807 + 1`, `-9223372036854775808 / -1`, and `- -9223372036854775808` are 9.2233720368547758e+18. A division by zero gives NULL, also `5.0 / 0` and `null / 0`. NULL in any operand gives NULL. s4ql refuses an overflow and a division by zero, which can depend on the rows and the arguments; when it refuses them is the open item of [expressions](expressions.md).
  - **Text and blob columns and arguments.** SQLite computes with a TEXT column or a text argument as a number: `s + 1` is 5 for `s = '4'`, and `x'35' + 1` is 6. The agreed rule refuses arithmetic on text, so the engine refuses a column of type TEXT or BLOB in an expression, which it knows from the schema, and an argument of a `?` in an expression that is a text or a blob, which it knows only when it runs.
  - **Comparison with a column applies the column's affinity.** An arithmetic result has no affinity, so `s = 3 + 1`, with `s` TEXT and `'4'`, chooses the row: SQLite converts 4 to `'4'`. By the agreed rule a comparison of values of other types is refused, so a TEXT or BLOB column compared with an expression must be refused; INT, INTEGER, and REAL compare numerically, as `i = 1.5 * 2` chooses `i = 3`.
  - **SET into a STRICT column converts.** `set s = i + 1` stores the text `'4'`, `set i = r * 2` stores 5 where `r * 2` is 5.0, `set i = r` refuses 2.5 with `cannot store REAL value in INT column t.i`, and `set r = i + 1` stores 6.0. By the exact types of slice 4, a value of another type than its column is refused, so the engine must decide whether an expression of INT columns may go into a REAL column; the type of the value depends on its operands, and on the arguments.
  - **The column of a condition.** A condition on a column alone with an expression without a column can serve the key or an index as a `?` does; one with a column is a filter. A name in an expression of a subquery may be a column of the statement, as for slice 15, and is then correlated.
