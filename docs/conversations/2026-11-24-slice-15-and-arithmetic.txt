  ## Slice 15, statement pass: report

  The statement pass now reads a scalar subquery. The engine and the driver
  refuse a statement with one until the engine step. `make test`, `make
  test-sql` (no `.tsv` changed) and `make lint` pass. After the review fixes I
  re-ran lint, the short tests of the changed packages, and the simulator
  package with `-tags s4ql_browser`. FuzzCheck ran 30 s and FuzzGenerate 20 s,

  **Branch:** `slice-15-stmt`, last commit `febc2da`.

  ### Typed form (contract for the engine)
  - `Select`, `Update` and `Delete` get `Subqueries []stmt.Select`: the
  subqueries of the WHERE, in text order, or nil.
  - A condition's value names one by `Term.Subquery`, numbered from 1, the way
  `Term.Parameter` names an argument.
    - Its `Value` is nil, its `Parameter` is 0, and its `Span` covers the
  parentheses.
  - I used a list and a number, not a pointer, because the package's rule is
  that no statement holds a pointer. The tests and the generator compare
  statements with `%#v`.
  - The inner `Select`:
    - `Span` runs from its SELECT to its last token.
    - `Columns` holds one name, or is nil for `*`.
    - It has `Where`, `OrderBy` and `Limit`, and its own `Subqueries` is nil.
  - Parameters are numbered over the whole statement in text order. There is now
  one counter, `recognizer.parameters`, instead of a count passed to each
  function. `Parameters()` includes the subqueries' parameters.

  ### What is accepted
  - A subquery as the value of a comparison in the WHERE of a select, an update
  or a delete.
  - Either side of the column, all six operators and all eight spellings. One
  function, `recognizer.conditionValue`, reads every comparison's value, so this
  is uniform.
  - The inner select: one table; one column or `*`; WHERE, ORDER BY, LIMIT; `?`
  parameters.
  - goqite's receive without `received + 1` is accepted.

  ### What is refused (`s4ql: not supported: <name> (at offset N)`)
  - **New names:**
    - `subquery in a subquery`, at the inner parenthesis.
    - `subquery of more than one column`, at the second column.
    - `subquery after a sign`, at the parenthesis, for `-(select …)`. `+(select
  …)` gives `+ sign`.
  - **Existing names:**
    - `IN` for `in (select …)` and `not in (select …)`.
    - `EXISTS`, and `NOT operator` for `not exists`.
    - `subquery in FROM`, also inside a subquery.
    - `compound SELECT` at the UNION, UNION ALL, EXCEPT or INTERSECT.
    - Select list and RETURNING: `result column other than a column or *`.
    - SET and VALUES: `subquery`.
    - ORDER BY: `ordering term other than a column`.
    - LIMIT: `LIMIT of an expression`.
    - DEFAULT: `default ((select 1))` gives `DEFAULT of an expression`. `default
  (select 1)` is SQLite's syntax error.
    - A subquery alone as a condition: `subquery`.
    - Before IS: `value before IS`.
    - After IS: `IS with a value other than NULL`. This was `subquery` before.
    - Compared with a value or another subquery: `value compared with a value`.
    - `((select …))`: `parentheses`.
    - COLLATE: `COLLATE`.
    - Inside the parentheses every refusal of a select keeps its name.
  - **Depth:** `WHERE deeper than SQLite's limit` covers the subquery cases. The
  details are under the stricter/different list below.
  - **Until the engine step:** `engine.subqueryRefusal` gives `s4ql: not
  supported: subquery` at the first subquery's opening parenthesis.
    - `NewPlan` panics on such a statement.
    - The driver's `check` calls `engine.Check` again, so `Prepare` refuses it.

  ### Where s4ql is stricter than or different from SQLite (for Markus)
  - **Two or more named columns:** not supported. SQLite gives `row value
  misused`, or `no such table` first. Both are errors, so only the message
  differs. This follows the precedent of `index beyond SQLite's limit`.
  - **Depth checked at name resolution:**
    - Once SQLite has found the tables, it adds the height of the outer WHERE to
  the height of the subquery's WHERE. So the subquery's WHERE allows at most
  498 conditions, or 497 inside an AND. The parser alone allows 997.
    - The pass refuses this at the subquery. SQLite gives `Expression tree is
  too large` on a database with the tables, and `no such table` on an empty one.
    - Parse-time limits also apply: from 998 conditions at the token after the
  comparison, 999 at `)`, 1,000 at the inner AND. An AND made too deep by a
  subquery on its right is refused at the token after it.
  - **Changed messages for texts that were already refused:**
    - `v is (select …)` now says `IS with a value other than NULL`.
    - A text whose first refused token is inside a subquery, or after it (`… =
  (select k from u) or v = 1` now gives `OR`), gets that token's error instead
  of `subquery`.
  - With many subqueries, `parameter beyond SQLite's limit` can now occur in a
  WHERE. No test covers that case.

  ### Left for the engine step (open items in `docs/todo/sql-front-end.md`,
  facts in `docs/todo/slice-15.md`)
  - **Correlated subqueries** are not detected. SQLite silently takes a name the
  inner table lacks from the outer table.
    - The typed form has the inner `Table`, plus every name with its offset in
  `Columns`, `Where` and `OrderBy`.
    - The rule: a name the inner table lacks and the outer table has is
  correlated. A name neither has is `no such column`. If both are the same
  table, nothing is correlated.
    - I recommend refusing correlated subqueries.
  - **Design question for you/Markus:** which row gives the value when the
  subquery's rows have no order, or tie on its ORDER BY. SQLite takes the first
  row it reads. The spec's rule says unspecified order varies, and goqite's
  ORDER BY can tie.
  - **`limit ?` inside a subquery:** SQLite reads LIMIT X as LIMIT (X <> 0). So
  `'x'`, `-1` and `1.5` give the first row, 0 and 0.0 give none, and NULL gives
  `datatype mismatch`.
  - **Error texts and order for `*` of more than one column:**
    - In a select: `row value misused`.
    - In an update or a delete: `sub-select returns N columns - expected 1`. The
  exception is `!=` or `<>` with the subquery on the left, which gives `row
  value misused`.
  - **Order of errors:** a select finds every table before any column. An update
  or a delete finds the subquery's table only when its WHERE walk reaches the
  subquery.
  - **Evaluation:**
    - SQLite computes the value once, from the rows before the statement changes
  any.
    - It computes it only when needed, so whether a runtime error in the
  subquery shows depends on the plan.
  - **Test helpers that drop subqueries:** `stmttest.SelectOf`, `WhereCases`,
  `orderedOf`, and drivercheck's `reachChange`. `reach`, `everyPath` and
  `pair.step` skip subquery statements for now. Only generated sequences reach
  those helpers, and `Sequence` writes no subqueries.
  - `checkSubqueries` compares sorted rows. No test checks the order of a select
  with both ORDER BY and a subquery against SQLite.

⏺ The feature map is on main: docs/research/feature-map.md. Six core gaps,
  ordered by how many features wait on each: expressions (23), query shapes
  (18), schema (13), writes (6), driver (5), and limits of the core.

  First decision, on expressions, the largest gap: where are expressions 
  evaluated?

  - (a) An evaluator of our own in Go, checked against SQLite with generated
    expressions.
  - (b) In the cache's SQLite, in statements that s4ql writes, as the cache
    sorts rows today.

  I recommend (a). With (b), SQLite computes the answer and is also the thing we
  check the answer against, so the differential tests stop telling us anything
  about expressions. It also bends the spec's rule that SQLite does not run the
  application's SQL, and it takes time and randomness out of the host's hands,

❯ What do other storage engines do?                                             

⏺ From memory, not checked against their documentation today:

  - SQLite. Sorting and grouping use a temporary B-tree that starts in memory
    and spills to a temporary file. Rows of a returning are different: they are
    all accumulated in memory before the first one is given, with no bound,
    which is what s4ql does today.
  - PostgreSQL. A budget per operation (work_mem, 4 MB by default). Sorts, hash
    joins, and hash aggregates spill to disk above it. An error comes only if a
    cap on temporary files is set.
  - MySQL. A sort buffer and in-memory temporary tables up to a set size, then
    on disk.
  - DuckDB. A memory limit for the whole process. Most operators spill to a
    temporary directory; what cannot spill fails with an out-of-memory error.
  - CockroachDB. A budget per operator (64 MB), then spill to disk, and a
    "memory budget exceeded" error where spilling is not possible.
  - TiDB. A quota per query (1 GB). Above it the query is cancelled with an
    error, or spills for the operators that can.

  The pattern: mature engines have both, a budget with spilling and an error as
  the backstop. The younger ones, CockroachDB and TiDB, started with the error
  and added spilling one operator at a time.

  That matches (b) first and (a) later. Do you want that order?

  ### When an evaluation error comes
  - **Expression without a column:** computed once per run, in text order (SET,
  then WHERE, then each subquery), after the names, the lengths, the refusals of
  SET's expressions, and WHERE's type checks, and before the access is chosen.
  It serves a key lookup or index bounds as an argument does, and NULL means no
  row.
    - Its error comes whatever the rows: on an empty table, with LIMIT 0, or
  beside a condition that is never true.
    - It is not computed in a read-only transaction, where SQLite's readonly
  error comes first.
    - A subquery's constants are computed only when the subquery is.
    - The type refusal of a SET value and its NOT NULL check (in `update.check`)

  - **Expression with a column:** computed for each row the access reads (WHERE)
  or the statement changes (SET). It never serves the access and is never
  rewritten.
    - A row's conditions are evaluated in text order and stop at the first that
  is not true, like `&&`.
    - Both sides of one comparison are computed, even when the first is NULL.
    - An update evaluates its WHERE on every row it reads before it changes any.
  Then it computes SET for each row in key order, from the row as it was before
  the statement.
  - **Where the outcome depends on the access or the draw:**
    - A row the access does not read is not evaluated, so a condition that
  serves as the key lookup or a key or index bound guards every other condition,
  wherever it stands. On a table where `b` is 0 in the row `k = 1`, `a / b > 1
  and k > 1` gives no error by the key range, and fails on a full scan.
    - With LIMIT, the drawn direction decides whether a failing row is reached.
  `select k from t where 10 / (k - 1) > 0 limit 1` gives row 4 when read
  backward and a division by zero when read forward. Subqueries behave the same
  way.
  - **A failed statement changes nothing:** a statement of its own is rolled
  back, one in a transaction rolls back to its savepoint and the transaction
  stays usable, and RETURNING gives no rows. An evaluation error leaves the
  count of changes, as "not supported" does.

  primary key of several rows, SQLite collects the keys in a temporary index
  ordered by the key and changes the rows in that order, whatever index it
  scanned. That is the same order s4ql uses. The pass refuses `primary key desc`
